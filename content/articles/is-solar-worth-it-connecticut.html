Is solar worth it in Connecticut? For most homeowners, yes. Connecticut has some of the highest electricity prices in the United States, and its Residential Renewable Energy Solutions (RRES) program offers two ways to get paid, including a fixed rate for all your solar production that is locked for 20 years. The main decision is which tariff to choose.

RRES: Netting Tariff vs Buy-All Tariff

Connecticut ended classic net metering for new customers in 2022 and replaced it with RRES, run by Eversource and United Illuminating under the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA). You choose one tariff and keep it for 20 years:

  • Netting Tariff. Solar powers your home first, and exports earn retail-rate credits that carry forward. For customers enrolling in 2026, a Solar Energy Adjustment of about 4 cents is charged on every kWh your system produces, which trims savings.
  • Buy-All Tariff. All of your production is sold to the utility at a fixed rate, about 32.9 cents per kWh for 2026 enrollees, for 20 years. You keep buying all your power at the normal retail rate. This option is common for leases and PPAs, but homeowners can choose it too. Payments may be taxable income, so ask a tax professional.

Batteries and Other Connecticut Incentives

  • Energy Storage Solutions. Connecticut's battery program pays incentives for storage paired with solar, now structured as a smaller upfront enrollment payment plus performance payments for letting the utility use your battery at peak times. It was restructured in April 2026, so check the current terms. See our battery cost guide.
  • Sales tax exemption. Residential solar equipment is exempt from Connecticut's sales tax.
  • Property tax exemption. The added value of residential solar is exempt from local property tax.
  • Green Bank financing. The Connecticut Green Bank supports low-cost solar loans through local lenders.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Connecticut still have net metering?

Not for new customers. Connecticut uses the RRES program, with a Netting Tariff that credits exports at retail and a Buy-All Tariff that pays a fixed rate for all production.

Which is better, Netting or Buy-All?

At 2026 rates, Buy-All often produces a faster payback for a purchased system, while Netting keeps your bill simpler and benefits more if retail rates rise quickly. Ask installers to model both for your home.

What is the Solar Energy Adjustment?

A charge of about 4 cents per kWh on all solar production for Netting Tariff customers who enroll in 2026. It does not apply to the Buy-All Tariff.

How long does solar take to pay off in Connecticut?

Roughly 7.5 to 10.5 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on the tariff and your installed price.
