Is solar worth it in Iowa? In 2026 it usually is. Iowa's two big utilities still credit exported solar at about the retail energy rate, and a 2020 state law locks that compensation in for 20 years for systems installed before a new value-of-solar method arrives. That method is due by mid-2027, so homeowners who are already considering solar have a reason not to wait too long.

How Solar Billing Works in Iowa

Senate File 583, passed in 2020, requires MidAmerican Energy and Alliant Energy (Interstate Power and Light) to offer net billing or inflow-outflow billing to customers with solar. Both use inflow-outflow billing:

  • Inflow is the power you buy from the grid, billed at your normal rate.
  • Outflow is the solar you send to the grid. It earns a credit at the retail energy rate, including most riders, so exports are worth nearly as much as solar you use at home.
  • Credits reduce energy charges but not the fixed monthly customer charge, and leftover credits are not paid out in cash, so size your system to your use.

The law also requires a value-of-solar methodology to take effect by July 1, 2027, or sooner if distributed generation reaches 5% of statewide demand. The new method could pay new customers less. Customers who install before it applies keep their current compensation for 20 years. Municipal utilities and rural co-ops set their own rules. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Who Should Go Solar in Iowa

Iowa solar makes the most sense for Alliant customers, homes with good south-facing roofs, and owners who plan to stay 15 years or more. Panels still work in Iowa winters, and snow usually slides off; see solar in cloudy and snowy weather. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Iowa have net metering?

MidAmerican and Alliant use inflow-outflow billing instead. It works much like net metering because exported solar earns a credit at the retail energy rate.

What changes in Iowa in 2027?

State law requires a value-of-solar methodology by July 1, 2027, or sooner if solar reaches 5% of statewide demand. Systems installed before then keep their current compensation for 20 years.

Is there an Iowa solar tax credit?

No. Iowa's state solar tax credit ended for systems placed in service after 2021, and the federal homeowner credit ended after December 31, 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Iowa?

About 12–14 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility and roof.
