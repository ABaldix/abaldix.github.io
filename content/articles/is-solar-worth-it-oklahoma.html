Is solar worth it in Oklahoma? For many homeowners, yes. Electricity is cheaper than the national average, but Oklahoma gets plenty of sun, and its net metering rules net your solar against your use over a whole month. That keeps most of your solar worth the full retail rate. The catch is that monthly surplus earns only a few cents, so a system sized to your use pays off best.

How Net Metering Works in Oklahoma

Oklahoma Corporation Commission rules require utilities such as OG&E and Public Service Company of Oklahoma (PSO) to offer net metering for small systems. The key detail is the monthly window:

  • Within the month, the solar you export is netted against the power you buy. If your panels produce less than you use over the billing period, every solar kWh effectively saves the retail energy rate, even if you exported it at noon and bought it back at night.
  • Surplus at the end of the month is credited in dollars at the utility's avoided cost. It is based on wholesale power prices, changes every month, and is usually a few cents per kWh, far below retail. At OG&E, unused credits carry forward and are paid out after 24 months.
  • Fixed charges such as the monthly customer charge are not reduced by solar.

Rural electric cooperatives have their own terms, so co-op members should check their tariff. Oklahoma law also lets utilities ask regulators for a separate rate class for new solar customers. OG&E proposed a demand charge for solar homes in 2015 that was not approved, but it is worth watching future rate cases. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Who Should Go Solar in Oklahoma

Oklahoma solar suits homeowners with a sunny, south- or west-facing roof, high summer air-conditioning bills, and plans to stay at least 12 years. Size the system to your actual yearly use, or a bit below it, so little energy ends up as cheap monthly surplus. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Oklahoma have net metering?

Yes. OG&E and PSO net your solar against your use over each monthly billing period. Surplus left at the end of the month earns the utility's avoided cost, a wholesale-based price that is usually a few cents per kWh.

Are there solar tax credits in Oklahoma?

There is no state tax credit or rebate for residential solar in 2026, and the 30% federal homeowner credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Oklahoma?

About 11–13 years for a purchased system sized to your use, based on 2026 prices and Oklahoma's average electricity rate.

Should I oversize my system in Oklahoma?

No. Solar beyond your monthly use is worth only the avoided-cost rate, so extra panels add cost but little savings.
