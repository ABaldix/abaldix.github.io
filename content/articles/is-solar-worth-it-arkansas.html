Is solar worth it in Arkansas? It can be, but the rules changed. Since Act 278 took effect, new rooftop solar no longer earns the full retail rate for power sent to the grid. Solar still saves the full price on every kWh you use at home, so the best Arkansas systems are smaller ones sized to your daytime use.

How Net Billing Works in Arkansas

Act 278 of 2023, the Arkansas Cost-Shifting Prevention Act, ended 1:1 net metering for new customers. Homes that submitted interconnection paperwork by September 30, 2024 kept "legacy" retail-rate credits, which under state rules continue until June 1, 2040. Everyone who applies after that date takes service under the new rules. At Entergy Arkansas, the state's largest utility, the Non-Legacy Net-Metering schedule works like this:

  • Power you use while the panels produce never passes through the meter, so it saves the full retail rate.
  • Power you buy and power you export are measured separately. You pay the normal rate for every kWh you buy, even if you exported more earlier that day.
  • Exports are credited at avoided cost, a wholesale-based price set each year. It is 3.34¢ per kWh from March 2026 through February 2027, up from 2.47¢ the year before.
  • Credits are applied in dollars to your bill and carry forward, but they cannot reduce the customer charge or minimum bill. There is also a one-time interconnection fee of $196.75 for a typical home system.

SWEPCO, OG&E's Arkansas service area, municipal utilities, and electric cooperatives follow the same law but file their own avoided-cost rates and terms, so check your utility's tariff. Read net metering vs. net billing for the basics.

What About a Battery?

A battery lets you store midday solar and use it in the evening instead of exporting it for 3.34¢. In Arkansas that can raise the value of each stored kWh by about 11¢, but a battery costs $10,000–$15,000 or more, so it rarely pays for itself on savings alone. It makes more sense if you want backup power for storms and outages. See solar with or without a battery.

Consumer Protections Under Act 278

Act 278 also added rules for solar sellers. Before you sign, a seller must give you at least five working days to review the proposal and provide a written estimate of system output, total cost, payment schedule, payback, and projected bill savings, plus warranty details. They must also offer or explain how to get an energy-efficiency audit and tell you that you can file a complaint with the Arkansas Attorney General. Entergy Arkansas has warned customers about salespeople who falsely claim to work with the utility. Our guide to choosing a solar installer covers the other checks.

Who Should Go Solar in Arkansas

Arkansas solar suits homeowners with a sunny, south- or west-facing roof, high daytime use such as summer air conditioning or working from home, and plans to stay 15 years or more. Ask installers to size the system to your daytime use rather than your total yearly use, and compare at least three quotes with our quote checklist. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Arkansas still have net metering?

Only for legacy customers who applied by September 30, 2024. New customers get net billing: exports are credited at the utility's avoided cost, 3.34¢ per kWh at Entergy Arkansas through February 2027.

Are there solar tax credits in Arkansas?

No. Arkansas has no state tax credit, rebate, or sales or property tax exemption for home solar, and the 30% federal homeowner credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Arkansas?

About 14–17 years for a purchased system sized to cover 40–60% of your use, based on 2026 prices. A system sized to cover all your use takes about 21 years.

What size solar system should I get in Arkansas?

Smaller than you might think. Because exports earn only avoided cost, size the system to the electricity you use while the sun is shining, often 40–60% of your yearly use.
