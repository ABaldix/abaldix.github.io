Is solar worth it in Missouri? It can be, but it is a closer call than in most states. Missouri's electricity is cheaper than average, the federal credit is gone, and most utility rebates have ended. Whether solar pays off depends heavily on your utility: Evergy customers in the Kansas City area generally do better than Ameren customers around St. Louis, and Columbia residents still get a generous city rebate.

How Net Metering Works in Missouri

Missouri's Net Metering and Easy Connection Act requires every utility, including co-ops and municipal utilities, to offer net metering for customer-owned systems up to 100 kW. Within each billing month, your solar offsets what you buy from the grid at the full retail rate. The difference is what happens to monthly surplus. Ameren Missouri credits it at its avoided cost, reported at about 5.4¢/kWh in summer and 3.9¢ in winter, while Evergy's net metering is reported to credit surplus more generously. Because sunny months often produce surplus, Ameren customers should size a system close to their own use rather than larger. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Missouri Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Columbia Water & Light rebate. Columbia's municipal utility pays $500 per kW of solar, the only major residential solar rebate still open in Missouri.
  • Closed utility rebates. Ameren Missouri, Evergy, and Liberty offered rebates of about $0.25 per watt, but those programs closed around 2023. Some websites still list them; confirm with your utility.
  • Solar access law. Missouri law limits homeowners associations from banning rooftop solar outright, though they can set reasonable rules.
  • Federal credit. The 30% residential credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025. Leases may still pass along a commercial credit in a lower price.

Who Should Go Solar in Missouri

Solar is a solid fit for Columbia residents, Evergy customers, and homeowners who use a lot of power during the day. Ameren customers can still come out ahead over 25 years, but should keep the system modest, get several quotes, and push for a price under $2.85 per watt. A battery can help Ameren customers store midday surplus instead of selling it cheaply; see solar with or without a battery. For a neighboring state with richer incentives, read our Illinois guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Missouri have net metering?

Yes. All Missouri utilities must offer net metering for systems up to 100 kW. Solar offsets your use at retail within each month, but Ameren credits monthly surplus at a lower avoided-cost rate.

Are there solar rebates in Missouri in 2026?

Columbia Water & Light pays $500 per kW. The Ameren, Evergy, and Liberty solar rebates closed around 2023.

Is solar better with Ameren or Evergy?

Evergy customers generally see faster payback because surplus is reported to be credited more generously. Ameren customers should size systems close to their own use.

How long does solar take to pay off in Missouri?

About 12 to 15 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, and closer to 10 years in Columbia with the city rebate.
