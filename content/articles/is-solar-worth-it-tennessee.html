Is solar worth it in Tennessee? For pure bill savings, it is one of the tougher states in the country. Tennessee power is cheap, the state has no net metering, and the federal credit is gone for 2026 installs. Solar can still be a reasonable long-term choice if you size it to your daytime use, plan to stay in your home for 20 years, or value backup power during storms.

Why Tennessee Has No Net Metering

Nearly all Tennessee homes get power from a local power company, such as Nashville Electric Service, KUB, EPB, or MLGW, that buys its electricity from the Tennessee Valley Authority (TVA). TVA is a federal corporation, and it does not offer retail-rate net metering. Instead, it buys solar exports through its Dispersed Power Production (DPP) program at its avoided cost. TVA's January 2026 guidelines list a flat rate of about 4.4¢/kWh, with time-of-use prices from the mid-3¢ range to about 5¢.

Not every local power company makes the same options available, so ask yours how exports are handled before you sign. Read how net metering and net billing differ for the basics.

Tennessee Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Property tax break. Certified green energy property is assessed at no more than 12.5% of its installed cost, which keeps the tax bump small.
  • TVA EnergyRight financing. Some local power companies offer low-interest loans for home energy upgrades.
  • No state tax credit or rebate for residential solar.
  • Federal credit. The 30% residential credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

Who Should Go Solar in Tennessee

Solar makes the most sense for Tennessee homeowners who want backup power, plan to stay long-term, or use a lot of electricity during the day. Pairing a small system with a battery lets you keep the lights on during storms; see whether solar works in a power outage. If savings are your only goal, an energy audit, insulation, or a heat pump will often pay back faster. For a nearby state with similar rules, read our Georgia guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Tennessee have net metering?

No. TVA and the local power companies that sell its electricity do not offer net metering. Exports can be sold at a low avoided-cost rate through TVA's Dispersed Power Production program where available.

How much does TVA pay for solar?

TVA's January 2026 Dispersed Power Production guidelines list a flat rate of about 4.4¢/kWh, with time-of-use prices from the mid-3¢ range to about 5¢.

Are there solar incentives in Tennessee?

Mainly a property tax break that assesses solar at no more than 12.5% of its cost. There is no state tax credit or rebate for homeowners.

How long does solar take to pay off in Tennessee?

About 15–20 years in 2026, depending on system size and how much solar power you use directly.
