Is solar worth it in Alabama? For most homeowners, it is a hard case in 2026. Alabama has decent sun and mid-range power prices, but there is no net metering, exported solar earns only a few cents, and Alabama Power adds a monthly fee based on the size of your system. A small system that you use almost entirely at home can still pay off slowly. A big system usually does not.

How Solar Billing Works in Alabama

Alabama is one of the few states with no net metering rule. What you get depends on your utility:

  • Alabama Power (most of central and southern Alabama). Solar you use at home cuts your bill at the full rate. Power you send to the grid is bought under Rate PAE at the company's avoided cost, a few cents per kWh. On top of that, solar customers pay a capacity reservation charge of $5.41 per kW each month, about $27 a month for a 5 kW system. Customers and the Southern Environmental Law Center sued over the fee, but a federal judge ruled for Alabama Power and the state Public Service Commission on March 25, 2026.
  • TVA areas (Huntsville, Decatur, and much of North Alabama). Local power companies follow TVA's rules, with no net metering. Exports earn a low credit, but the Alabama Power fee does not apply. Our Tennessee guide explains TVA's approach.
  • Electric cooperatives set their own terms, so check with your co-op before getting quotes.

Who Should Go Solar in Alabama

Solar in Alabama is mostly for homeowners who value energy independence or backup power, not quick savings. If you go ahead, keep the system small and aim to use nearly all of its output at home, for example by running laundry, pool pumps, or an EV charger during the day. A battery raises self-use and gives outage protection, but it adds cost; see solar with or without a battery. Homes in TVA areas avoid the Alabama Power fee. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Alabama have net metering?

No. Alabama has no statewide net metering. Alabama Power buys exported solar at its avoided cost, roughly 3.5–5.5¢ per kWh.

What is the Alabama Power solar fee?

It is a capacity reservation charge of $5.41 per kW of system size per month. A federal judge upheld it in March 2026.

Are there solar incentives in Alabama?

There is no state tax credit or rebate for home solar, and the federal homeowner credit ended after December 31, 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Alabama?

About 16–19 years for a small system used mostly on site. A full-size Alabama Power system may not pay back within 25 years.
