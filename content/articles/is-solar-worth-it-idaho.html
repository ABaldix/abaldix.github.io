Is solar worth it in Idaho? It can be, but it takes patience. Idaho has good sunshine and some of the cheapest electricity in the US, and most new solar customers are on Idaho Power's net billing, which pays well for summer evening exports and very little the rest of the year. The state tax deduction helps a bit. A system sized for self-use typically pays back in about 14 to 16 years; a full-size one takes closer to 20.

How Net Billing Works in Idaho

Idaho has no statewide net metering law; each utility's rules are set by the Idaho Public Utilities Commission (IPUC).

  • Idaho Power (Boise, Treasure Valley, Magic Valley). Since 2024, new solar customers use net billing. Solar you use at home saves the full rate, and exports earn a dollar credit that depends on season and time. In a September 2025 order, effective October 1, the IPUC limited the drop in the energy part of the credit to 40%, less than Idaho Power had asked for, and froze the rates until 2028. Exports during summer on-peak hours (3 to 11 p.m., Monday through Saturday, June through September) earn about 15.7¢, but most midday and non-summer exports earn about 3¢. Unused credits carry forward in dollars. Customers who installed by December 20, 2019 keep legacy net metering through 2045.
  • Rocky Mountain Power (East Idaho). In late 2025 it asked the IPUC to move new customers to a time-varying export credit averaging about 4¢ per kWh. Check the current status before you buy.
  • Avista (North Idaho) and co-ops have their own terms, so check your utility's current tariff.

Who Should Go Solar in Idaho

Idaho solar works best for homeowners who use a lot of power during the day, plan to stay 20 years or more, and size the system to cover part of their use rather than all of it. Because summer on-peak exports earn about five times more than other exports, west-facing panels that produce more in the late afternoon can add value; see solar panel orientation and tilt. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Idaho have net metering?

Not for new Idaho Power customers. Since 2024 they are on net billing, with export credits that vary by season and time. Customers who installed by December 20, 2019 keep legacy net metering through 2045.

What does Idaho Power pay for solar exports?

About 15.7¢ per kWh on summer on-peak hours, 3.4¢ on summer off-peak hours, and 2.9¢ the rest of the year, under the rates in effect since October 2025. They are frozen until 2028.

Is there an Idaho solar tax incentive?

Yes. Idaho Code 63-3022C allows a state income tax deduction of 40% of the cost in the first year and 20% in each of the next three years, up to $5,000 a year. It is a deduction, not a credit, so it saves a few hundred dollars a year.

How long does solar take to pay off in Idaho?

About 14–16 years for a system sized for self-use, and about 20 years for one that covers all your use.
