Is solar worth it in Colorado? For most homeowners with a sound, sunny roof, yes. Colorado's high elevation brings strong sunshine, and cool temperatures help panels convert more of it into electricity. The state's biggest utility still offers net metering, and Colorado does not charge state sales tax or extra property tax on solar. The main local risk is hail, which you can plan for.

How Net Metering Works in Colorado

Colorado law requires investor-owned utilities to offer net metering, and Xcel Energy, which serves Denver and much of the Front Range, credits exported solar against power you use later. Xcel's residential customers are on time-of-use pricing, so solar produced in the afternoon can be worth more than midday production. Rural electric co-ops set their own rules, and some credit exports at less than retail, so check yours before you size a system. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Colorado Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Sales and use tax exemption. Solar and storage equipment is exempt from Colorado's 2.9% state sales tax. Some cities and counties also waive or rebate their local sales tax; Boulder is one example.
  • Property tax exemption. The added value of a residential solar system is excluded from property tax.
  • Xcel Energy Solar*Rewards. Xcel's programs have shifted over the years and now focus largely on income-qualified customers. Ask your installer what is currently offered.
  • Community solar gardens. If your roof does not work, Colorado has an active community solar market.
  • No federal credit for purchases. It ended after 2025; leases and PPAs can still pass through the commercial credit. See lease vs. buy.

Planning for Hail and Snow

Colorado's Front Range is one of the most hail-prone regions in the country. Most quality panels are tested against one-inch hail at high speed, and many withstand larger stones, but severe storms can still crack glass. Choose panels with strong hail ratings, confirm your homeowner's policy covers the array, and understand your deductible. Our hail and insurance guide covers what to ask. Snow is less of a worry: panels shed it quickly in Colorado's sunny winters.

Who Should Go Solar in Colorado

Solar is a strong fit for homeowners with a south- or west-facing roof in good condition, Xcel or Black Hills service, and plans to stay around ten years or more. If your roof is near the end of its life, replace it first, ideally with impact-resistant shingles, which can also lower insurance premiums.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Colorado have net metering?

Yes. Investor-owned utilities including Xcel Energy and Black Hills Energy offer net metering. Electric co-ops and municipal utilities set their own policies.

Do solar panels raise property taxes in Colorado?

No. Colorado excludes the value of residential solar energy equipment from property tax.

Will hail damage my solar panels in Colorado?

Most panels survive typical hail, but large stones can break them. Pick panels with high hail ratings and make sure your insurance covers the system.

How long does solar take to pay off in Colorado?

About 9 to 11 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your rate plan, installed price, and roof orientation.
