How much do solar panels cost? In 2026, most homeowners are quoted roughly $2.60 to $3.10 per watt before incentives, which puts a typical 8 kW system at about $21,000 to $25,000. Your price depends on system size, your roof, your state, the equipment, and above all on how you pay. This guide shows real 2026 numbers and how to tell whether a quote is fair.

Solar Panel Cost per Watt in 2026

Solar is priced per watt of panel capacity, because that makes quotes of different sizes comparable. Two large datasets give a good picture of 2026 prices:

  • Quotes: the median quote on the EnergySage marketplace was $2.57 per watt in the first half of 2026, up about 3% from late 2025. Solar-only quotes were about $2.62 per watt.
  • Installed systems: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory's latest data update found a median installed price of $3.00 per watt for cash-purchased home systems in 2025, and $3.60 per watt across all homeowner-owned systems.

Marketplace quotes come from installers competing for the job, so they tend to run lower than the average system installed across the country. We use $2.85 per watt as a middle-of-the-road figure in our guides and calculators. Prices among the 100 largest installers ranged from about $2.40 to $6.30 per watt in 2025, which is why getting several quotes matters so much.

What You Are Paying For

The panels themselves are a smaller share of the bill than most people expect. A quote typically covers:

  • Equipment: panels, inverters or microinverters, racking, and wiring. See best solar panels and inverter options.
  • Labor and design: engineering, the roof crew, and the electrician.
  • Permits, inspection, and interconnection with your utility.
  • Sales, marketing, and overhead: often a large part of the price, and the part that varies most between companies.

Because so much of the price is labor and overhead rather than hardware, two installers using the same panels can quote very different totals.

What a Battery Adds

Batteries are expensive. EnergySage reported a median battery price of about $1,130 per kWh of storage in the first half of 2026, so a 13.5 kWh battery adds roughly $15,000. Berkeley Lab found that cash-purchased solar-plus-storage systems had a median price of $5.10 per watt in 2025, versus $3.00 for solar alone. A battery makes the most sense where exports earn little or outages are common; see solar with or without a battery and solar battery cost.

How You Pay Changes the Price

The same system can carry a very different price depending on financing. In Berkeley Lab's 2025 data, the median loan-financed home system cost $4.50 per watt, compared with $3.00 for cash purchases. Much of that gap comes from "dealer fees" that lenders charge installers for low advertised interest rates, which are then built into the price. Always ask for the cash price, even if you plan to finance. Our guide to solar loans explains how to compare offers.

Leases and power purchase agreements (PPAs) work differently: the solar company owns the system and you pay monthly or per kWh. They became more common in 2026 because leasing companies can still claim a federal business tax credit; see solar lease vs. buy.

Incentives in 2026

The 30% federal tax credit for homeowners ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025, so if you buy, the price you see is close to the price you pay. Some states and utilities still offer rebates, tax credits, or tax exemptions. See our federal tax credit guide and solar incentives by state.

Where Prices May Go Next

Prices edged up in 2026 after the tax credit ended. EnergySage also points to new tariffs on imported polysilicon, effective December 2026, that could push panel costs higher in 2027, and battery prices have been rising as products adapt to new federal supply-chain rules. Waiting is unlikely to bring big savings; getting competitive quotes is the better lever.

How to Get a Fair Price

  • Get at least three quotes and compare them on price per watt (total cash price ÷ system watts).
  • Size the system to your usage, and to your state's export rules. Where exports earn little, a smaller system often pays back faster.
  • Check the production estimate against NREL's free PVWatts calculator.
  • Ask for the cash price next to any financing offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a solar panel system cost for an average house?

For a typical US home that needs about 8 kW, expect roughly $21,000–$25,000 before incentives in 2026, based on $2.60–$3.10 per watt.

Why are my solar quotes so different?

Labor, sales costs, and overhead make up most of the price and vary widely between companies. Equipment choices and financing also change the total. Compare quotes on price per watt using the cash price.

Is there still a solar tax credit in 2026?

Not for homeowners who buy. The 30% federal credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025. Leasing companies can still claim a business credit, and some states offer their own incentives.

How much does a solar battery add?

EnergySage reported a median of about $1,130 per kWh in early 2026, so a 13.5 kWh battery adds around $15,000.
