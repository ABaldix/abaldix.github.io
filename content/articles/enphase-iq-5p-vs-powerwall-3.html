Choosing between the Enphase IQ 5P vs Powerwall 3 is really a choice between two system designs. Enphase builds storage from small 5 kWh blocks, each with its own microinverters, that slot into Enphase's panel-level solar system. Tesla's Powerwall 3 is a single 13.5 kWh battery with a hybrid solar inverter built in. Both use safe lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells and both can back up a home, but they suit different situations.

Capacity and Power: How Many Units Do You Need?

A single IQ 5P is small. It can cover a refrigerator, lights, Wi-Fi, and a few circuits for several hours, but it cannot start a central air conditioner on its own. Most homes that want meaningful backup install two to four units. Three 5P batteries give you 15 kWh and about 11.5 kW of continuous power, roughly the same as one Powerwall 3.

The Powerwall 3 starts big. Its 11.5 kW continuous output is enough to start most central air conditioners and heat pumps, so many homes get partial or whole-home backup from one unit. Our battery sizing guide shows how to estimate what you need.

Cost: Enphase IQ 5P vs Powerwall 3 in 2026

Installed prices vary by market, but the pattern is consistent:

  • Powerwall 3: about $13,000 to $16,500 installed for one unit before incentives, and lower still in a new solar-plus-storage install because you do not need a separate solar inverter.
  • Enphase IQ 5P: about $15,000 installed for a typical two-unit (10 kWh) system, plus the IQ System Controller needed for backup. Matching a Powerwall's capacity with three units usually costs more.

Per kWh, the Powerwall 3 is usually cheaper. Enphase's advantage is that you can start small and add units later. See our full solar battery cost guide for incentives that may apply in your state, such as California's SGIP or Illinois's $300 per kWh rebate.

Which Works Better With Your Solar System?

If you already have Enphase microinverters, the IQ 5P is the natural choice. It is AC-coupled, uses the same monitoring app, and backup is managed by Enphase's system controller. Adding a Powerwall 3 to an existing system is possible, but it is designed around its own inverter.

If you are installing new solar and storage together, the Powerwall 3 is hard to beat on value. Panels connect directly to it, so the battery replaces the string inverter you would otherwise buy. That design has the trade-off of a single central inverter, discussed in our microinverters vs. string inverters guide.

If your roof is shaded or complex, an Enphase microinverter system with IQ batteries keeps panel-level optimization throughout. A Powerwall 3 system can use optimizers or rapid-shutdown devices to handle shade.

Reliability and Warranty

Enphase's 15-year warranty is one of the longest in the industry and its batteries have no fans or moving parts. The modular design also means one failed unit does not take your whole battery offline. Tesla's 10-year warranty is standard for the market, and its large installer network and software are mature. The IQ 5P meets the UL 9540A fire-propagation test criteria for indoor wall mounting; ask your installer for the certification of any battery you consider.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Enphase IQ 5P batteries equal one Powerwall 3?

About three. Three IQ 5P units provide 15 kWh and roughly 11.5 kW of continuous power, compared with 13.5 kWh and 11.5 kW for one Powerwall 3.

Can I add a Powerwall 3 to my Enphase solar system?

It is possible in some configurations, but the IQ Battery 5P integrates more simply with existing Enphase systems. Ask a certified installer which setup they support.

Which battery lasts longer?

Both use long-life LFP cells. Enphase warrants the IQ 5P for 15 years or 6,000 cycles; Tesla warrants the Powerwall 3 for 10 years.

Can either battery run my whole house?

One Powerwall 3 can back up many homes, depending on loads. With Enphase, whole-home backup typically takes three or more IQ 5P units and a properly sized system controller.
