Is solar worth it in Hawaii? Almost always, if you own a roof with good sun. Hawaii residents pay more than twice the national average for electricity, so every kWh your panels replace saves a lot of money. Net metering is long gone for new customers, but the combination of very high rates, strong sun, and a generous state tax credit still makes Hawaii one of the fastest payback markets in the country. About half of single-family homes on Oahu already have rooftop solar.

How Smart DER Works

Hawaii closed retail net metering to new customers in 2015. Since April 2024, new Hawaiian Electric rooftop solar customers on Oahu, Maui County, and Hawaii Island join the Smart Renewable Energy program, built on the Smart DER tariff:

  • Export option. Solar powers your home first, and exported power earns bill credits at time-differentiated rates set by the Public Utilities Commission. Midday exports are worth the least, because the grid already has plenty of solar then.
  • Non-Export option. Your system is designed not to send power to the grid at all, typically with a battery that soaks up surplus solar. Interconnection can be simpler.

Either way, the key to good savings in Hawaii is using your own solar, including after sunset. That is why most new Hawaii systems include a battery.

Hawaii Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Renewable Energy Technologies Income Tax Credit (RETITC). 35% of the installed cost, up to $5,000 per residential solar system, claimed on Form N-342. Most systems reach the cap.
  • Battery incentives. Hawaiian Electric's Battery Bonus closed to new applicants in 2024. Its BYOD Plus program has offered upfront incentives for batteries that the utility can call on at peak times, with higher amounts for income-qualified households. Check current terms.
  • Property tax. Hawaii counties generally exclude the value of residential solar systems from property tax assessments; confirm with your county.
  • No federal credit for purchases after 2025. Leases and PPAs can still pass through the commercial credit; compare in our lease vs. buy guide.

Tips for Hawaii Homeowners

  • Plan the battery from the start. A battery that covers your evening use usually delivers the best long-term savings. Compare options in our battery cost guide.
  • Mind salt air and wind. Ask for corrosion-resistant racking and panels rated for coastal environments, and confirm wind ratings for your area.
  • Expect interconnection review. Some circuits have limited hosting capacity, which can affect system size or require a Non-Export design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Hawaii have net metering?

Not for new customers. Hawaiian Electric's Smart DER program credits exported solar at time-based rates below retail, or lets you choose a Non-Export system. Older net metering customers keep their original terms.

How much is the Hawaii solar tax credit?

35% of the installed cost, up to $5,000 per residential solar system, through the Renewable Energy Technologies Income Tax Credit.

Do I need a battery with solar in Hawaii?

It is not required, but most new systems include one, because using your own solar in the evening is worth far more than exporting it at midday.

How long does solar take to pay off in Hawaii?

About 5 to 8 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, among the fastest in the US, thanks to very high electricity prices.
