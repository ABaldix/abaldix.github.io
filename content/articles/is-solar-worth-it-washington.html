Is solar worth it in Washington? It is a slower payback than in most states, and you should go in with clear eyes. Washington's hydropower keeps electricity cheap, and western Washington is famously cloudy from fall through spring. On the other hand, net metering still credits exports at full retail, and the state waives sales tax on solar. For homeowners who plan to stay a long time, or who are adding an EV or a heat pump, solar can still make sense.

How Net Metering Works in Washington

State law requires utilities to offer net metering, and Puget Sound Energy, Avista, Pacific Power, Seattle City Light, and most other utilities credit exports at the full retail rate. Credits roll over month to month, which lets summer surplus cover part of your winter bills. Any credits left on March 31 are reset each year, so size your system to your annual usage. A few public utilities pay less, and the law allows utilities to propose changes once statewide thresholds are reached, so confirm your utility's terms.

When Solar Makes Sense in Washington

  • You live east of the Cascades, where sunshine is much stronger.
  • Your usage is growing with an electric car or heat pump. See our solar + EV calculator.
  • Your utility's rates are rising or you are on a higher-priced plan.
  • You value energy independence and plan to stay 15+ years. Panels typically last 25–30 years; read how long solar panels last.

If your roof is shaded by tall evergreens, which is common around Puget Sound, solar is often not worth it. Our shading guide explains why.

Washington Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Sales and use tax exemption. Solar equipment and installation labor for systems up to 100 kW are exempt from state and local sales tax, worth roughly 6.5% to 10% of the cost. Your installer provides an exemption certificate.
  • Net metering, as described above.
  • Battery programs. Some utilities, including Puget Sound Energy, run programs that pay battery owners to help the grid at peak times.
  • No state income tax credit and no federal credit for purchases after 2025.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Washington have net metering?

Yes. Most Washington utilities credit exported solar at the full retail rate. Banked credits reset each year on March 31.

Do solar panels work in Seattle's cloudy weather?

Yes, but they produce much less from November to February. Annual production near Seattle is roughly a fifth lower than in eastern Washington, which lengthens payback.

Is there sales tax on solar panels in Washington?

No. Systems up to 100 kW are exempt from state and local sales tax through 2029.

How long does solar take to pay off in Washington?

About 14 to 17 years for a typical purchased system west of the Cascades in 2026, and noticeably less in sunnier eastern Washington.
