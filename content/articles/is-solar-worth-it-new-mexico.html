Is solar worth it in New Mexico? For most homeowners, yes. New Mexico's sunshine rivals Arizona's, its utilities still offer net metering, and it is one of the few states with a state solar tax credit that survived into 2026. Even without the federal credit, a well-sited system can pay for itself in under a decade.

The New Mexico Solar Tax Credit

The New Solar Market Development Income Tax Credit pays 10% of the purchase and installation cost of a home solar system, up to $6,000 per taxpayer. You apply to the state Energy, Minerals and Natural Resources Department for a certificate, then claim the credit on your state return. If the credit is larger than your tax bill, the rest is refunded, so you do not need a big state tax liability to use it. The program has a statewide annual cap, so apply soon after installation.

In 2026, Senate Bill 55 proposed raising the credit to 30% and the cap to $15,000. It passed the Senate in February but stalled in a House committee, so the 10% credit is what applies today. Lawmakers revisited the idea in an interim committee in September 2026, so watch the 2027 session.

How Net Metering Works in New Mexico

Under state rules, PNM, El Paso Electric, and Xcel Energy (Southwestern Public Service) must offer net metering to small solar systems. Your production first offsets what you use; surplus earns credits that roll forward on your bill, with the exact value set by each utility's tariff. Because New Mexico homes use a lot of power for summer cooling, most solar output is used or netted at close to full value. Co-op members should check their own co-op's rules. Who Should Go Solar in New Mexico

Nearly any homeowner with an unshaded roof is a good candidate, especially with high summer cooling bills. The strong sun lets a smaller, cheaper system cover your use, which keeps upfront costs down. Get at least three quotes and make sure each one includes the gross receipts tax exemption. Frequently Asked Questions

Does New Mexico have a solar tax credit in 2026?

Yes. The New Solar Market Development Tax Credit covers 10% of system cost, up to $6,000, and any amount above your tax bill is refunded.

Did New Mexico raise its solar credit to 30%?

Not yet. Senate Bill 55 would have raised it to 30% and $15,000, but it stalled in a House committee in 2026. It may return in the 2027 session.

Does PNM offer net metering?

Yes. PNM, El Paso Electric, and Xcel Energy offer net metering for small solar systems, with credit values set in each utility's tariff.

How long does solar take to pay off in New Mexico?

About 8 to 10 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, including the 10% state credit.
