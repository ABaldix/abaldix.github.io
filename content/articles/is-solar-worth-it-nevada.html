Is solar worth it in Nevada? It usually is, but more slowly than the sunshine suggests. Las Vegas and Reno get some of the strongest sun in the country, and NV Energy still offers a form of net metering that pays 75% of the retail rate for exported power. The catch is that Nevada electricity is among the cheapest in the West, so each kWh your panels replace is worth less than in states like California or New York.

How NV Energy Net Metering Works

Nevada restored net metering in 2017 with a tiered structure that stepped down as more solar was installed. New NV Energy customers are now in the final tier: excess solar you export earns a credit worth 75% of the retail rate. Power you use the moment it is produced still offsets 100% of the retail price. Systems must be 25 kW or smaller, which covers virtually every home.

Two details make this better than it sounds. First, the 75% is locked for 20 years from your interconnection date. Second, because the credit is a percentage of retail rather than a fixed amount, it rises automatically if NV Energy raises its rates. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Not on NV Energy? Rural co-ops such as Valley Electric Association set their own export rules, often less generous. Confirm your utility's policy before you sign.

How to Get More From Solar in Nevada

  • Use your power during the day. Every kWh you use directly is worth a third more than one you export. Pre-cool the house and run pool pumps in the afternoon.
  • Look at time-of-use plans. NV Energy's time-of-use rates charge more in summer afternoons and evenings. West-facing panels or a battery can shift value into those hours. See orientation and tilt.
  • Choose heat-tolerant panels. Roofs in Las Vegas get extremely hot, and panels lose output as they heat up. Compare temperature coefficients in our REC vs. Qcells comparison.
  • Keep panels clean. Desert dust can trim output a few percent between rains. An occasional rinse helps; read our maintenance guide.

Nevada Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Property tax exemption. Nevada law exempts the value a qualifying solar system adds to your home from property tax.
  • Net metering at 75% of retail, as described above.
  • No state income tax credit and no federal credit for purchased systems after 2025. Leases and PPAs can still pass through the commercial credit; compare carefully with our lease vs. buy guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Nevada have net metering?

Yes. NV Energy offers net metering for systems up to 25 kW, with exports credited at 75% of the retail rate for new customers, locked for 20 years.

How long does solar take to pay off in Nevada?

About 11 to 14 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your usage, rate plan, and installed price.

Do I need a battery with solar in Nevada?

Not for savings in most cases, because exports still earn 75% of retail. A battery makes more sense if you want backup power or are on a time-of-use plan with high evening prices.

Do solar panels raise property taxes in Nevada?

No. The value added by a qualifying solar energy system is exempt from Nevada property tax.
