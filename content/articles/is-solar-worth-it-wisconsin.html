Is solar worth it in Wisconsin? For many homeowners, yes. Wisconsin's electricity now costs more than the national average, every major utility offers net metering, and Focus on Energy raised its solar rebate sharply for 2026 to make up for the lost federal credit. The catch is that the value of your surplus solar depends on which utility serves you.

How Net Metering Works in Wisconsin

Wisconsin has no single statewide net metering rule; each utility's tariff, approved by the Public Service Commission, sets the terms. In general, your solar first offsets your own use within the month, and that part is worth the full retail rate. What changes is how each utility credits monthly surplus: some come close to retail, while others pay a lower buyback rate. Utilities have pushed to cut these credits, but the PSC rejected Madison Gas & Electric's proposal in 2023 and opened a broader study instead. Ask your installer to model your specific utility's tariff. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Wisconsin Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Focus on Energy rebate. For 2026, the statewide program raised its single-family home rebate to $600 per kW, up to $2,400, from a flat $300 in 2025. You reserve the rebate before installing, use a qualified contractor, and apply within 60 days of completion; the 2026 application deadline was August 31, 2026. Focus runs solar rebates every program year, so check the 2027 amounts before you sign. Your utility must participate in Focus on Energy.
  • Sales tax exemption. Solar equipment is exempt from Wisconsin sales tax.
  • Property tax exemption. The added value of solar is exempt from property tax.
  • Local programs. Milwaukee Shines offers solar financing for Milwaukee-area residents, and some co-ops and municipal utilities run their own incentives.

Who Should Go Solar in Wisconsin

Solar makes the most sense if you own your home, plan to stay a decade or more, and have an unshaded south-facing roof. Because surplus credits vary, homes that use more power during the day, or that add an electric vehicle or heat pump, capture more value. Cold, clear winter days are good for panel efficiency, and snow slides off tilted panels quickly. Frequently Asked Questions

How much is the Focus on Energy solar rebate?

For the 2026 program year, $600 per kW for single-family homes, up to $2,400. That replaced the flat $300 rebate from 2025. Check the 2027 terms before you install.

Does Wisconsin have net metering?

Yes. All major utilities offer net metering, but each sets its own terms, and the credit for monthly surplus varies from near retail to a lower buyback rate.

Is solar exempt from taxes in Wisconsin?

Yes. Solar equipment is exempt from sales tax, and the value solar adds to your home is exempt from property tax.

How long does solar take to pay off in Wisconsin?

About 10 to 13 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility's surplus credits and whether you get the Focus on Energy rebate.
