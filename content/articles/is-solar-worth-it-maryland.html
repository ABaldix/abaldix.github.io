Is solar worth it in Maryland? For most homeowners with a sunny roof, yes. Maryland has electricity prices well above the national average, full retail net metering, sales and property tax exemptions, and an SREC market that pays you for every megawatt-hour you produce. Even without the federal credit, that combination keeps payback periods under ten years for typical homes.

How Net Metering Works in Maryland

Maryland requires utilities, including BGE, Pepco, Delmarva Power, and Potomac Edison, to offer net metering. Exported solar is credited at the retail rate and offsets power you buy later. Unused credits carry forward, and any surplus at the annual true-up is settled at a lower rate, so size the system to your usage.

State law sets July 1, 2027 as the date by which the current net metering framework will be revisited, and a 2026 law clarified that timeline. Systems that interconnect under today's rules have typically been protected when states change their programs, but ask your installer to explain what applies to your contract. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Maryland SRECs

Maryland's renewable portfolio standard includes a solar carve-out, so electricity suppliers must buy solar renewable energy certificates (SRECs). Your system earns one SREC per 1,000 kWh produced, tracked in PJM-GATS, and you can sell them through a broker or let your installer handle it. Prices move with the market and have recently ranged from about $40 to $80. For a system producing 10,800 kWh a year, that is roughly $430 to $860 a year. SREC income is taxable.

Maryland Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Sales tax exemption. Residential solar equipment is exempt from Maryland's 6% sales tax.
  • Property tax exemption. The added value of a residential solar system is exempt from state and local property tax.
  • Income-qualified grants. The Maryland Energy Administration runs grant programs for eligible households that can cover thousands of dollars of system cost. The older $1,000 Residential Clean Energy Rebate has ended.
  • Battery grants. MEA's Residential and Commercial Energy Storage grant replaced the old storage tax credit. Check its current funding round.
  • County programs. Some counties, such as Montgomery County, offer property tax credits or green financing. Search DSIRE by ZIP code.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Maryland still have net metering?

Yes. Maryland utilities credit exported solar at the retail rate. State law sets a July 1, 2027 date for revisiting the program, so confirm which terms apply when you sign.

How much are Maryland SRECs worth?

They trade on the market and have recently been worth roughly $40 to $80 each. One SREC equals 1,000 kWh of solar production.

Is there a Maryland solar tax credit?

There is no general state income tax credit for solar in 2026, but there are sales and property tax exemptions and income-qualified grants.

How long does solar take to pay off in Maryland?

About 7.5 to 10 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on SREC prices, your utility rate, and installed price.
