Is solar worth it in Illinois? In most cases, yes, thanks to incentives rather than sunshine. Illinois has average sun and moved new solar customers off full net metering in 2025. But the state's Illinois Shines program and utility rebates can cover a large share of the cost upfront, which keeps payback periods competitive with much sunnier states.

What Changed With Net Metering in 2025

Systems that applied before January 1, 2025 kept full 1:1 net metering for the life of the system. New systems are on supply-only net metering: exported kWh earn credit for the supply portion of your bill, but not for delivery charges, taxes, and fees. In ComEd territory, the supply price was roughly 10¢ per kWh in summer 2025, compared with an all-in price near 19¢. The practical rule is the same as in other net billing states: power you use yourself is worth about twice as much as power you export.

To soften the change, the law created a one-time distributed generation rebate of $300 per kW for solar with a qualifying smart inverter, plus $300 per kWh for battery storage. ComEd battery rebate applicants must enroll in an hourly pricing supply plan.

How Illinois Shines Works

Illinois Shines, also called the Adjustable Block Program, buys the renewable energy credits (RECs) your system will generate over 15 years. One REC equals 1 MWh of production. For the 2025–2026 program year, small residential systems earned about $73.71 to $83.87 per REC depending on utility group. Systems of 10 kW or less are generally paid in full once they are energized. The payment usually flows through your installer, who may pass it to you as a lower price. Ask every installer to show the REC value and how much of it reaches you.

Other Illinois Incentives

  • Property tax treatment. Illinois assesses residential solar as if it were a conventional energy system, so it adds little or nothing to your property tax.
  • Illinois Solar for All. Income-qualified households can get solar with no upfront cost and guaranteed savings.
  • Community solar. Illinois has a large community solar market for renters and shaded homes.

Should You Add a Battery in Illinois?

A battery lets you store midday solar for the evening instead of exporting it at the supply rate, and the $300 per kWh rebate covers roughly a quarter to a third of its cost. It pays best for homes with large evening loads or frequent outages. Compare the numbers in our battery cost guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Illinois still have net metering?

Yes, but in a reduced form for new customers. Since 2025, exports are credited at the supply rate only. Systems that applied before 2025 keep full net metering.

How much is Illinois Shines worth?

For a typical residential system, often about $7,000 to $14,000 over its 15-year REC contract, depending on size and utility. Prices are reset each program year.

What is the ComEd solar rebate?

A one-time distributed generation rebate of $300 per kW of solar with a qualifying smart inverter, plus $300 per kWh for battery storage. Ameren offers the same rebate.

How long does solar take to pay off in Illinois?

About 8 to 10 years for a typical purchased system when you capture Illinois Shines and the utility rebate, and roughly 13 years or more without them.
