Is solar worth it in Georgia? For many homeowners, it is a long-term investment rather than a quick win. Georgia has solid sunshine and a fast-growing solar industry, but its largest utility pays a low rate for power you send back to the grid, and the state offers no solar tax credit. That does not rule solar out. It means the right system design, and the right expectations, matter more here than in most states.

How Georgia Power's Solar Buy Back Works

Georgia does not require utilities to offer full retail net metering. Georgia Power, which serves most of the state, uses a tariff called Renewable and Non-Renewable Instantaneous Netting, usually marketed as Solar Buy Back:

  • Solar you use instantly offsets your full retail price.
  • Any solar you are not using at that moment is counted as an export, even if you use more power later the same hour. There is no monthly netting.
  • Exports are credited at Georgia Power's solar avoided-cost rate, which was about 3.2¢ per kWh for 2026. Some analyses add a PSC-approved adder from the 2022 rate case, bringing the effective credit closer to 7¢. Either way, it is well under half of retail.
  • Residential systems are capped at 10 kW AC to participate.

Electric membership corporations (EMCs) such as Jackson EMC and Cobb EMC, and municipal utilities, write their own rules. Some are more generous, some less, so check yours first. Read how net metering works for the background.

When Solar Does Make Sense in Georgia

  • You use a lot of power during the day. Home offices, pool pumps, and daytime EV charging all raise the share of solar you use instantly, which is worth full retail.
  • You size for self-consumption. A smaller system matched to your daytime load exports less and earns a better return per panel than one sized to your whole annual bill.
  • You want backup power. A battery stores midday solar for the evening and keeps lights on during storms. In Georgia, a battery often matters more for resilience than for savings; see solar with or without a battery.
  • Your EMC offers better terms than Georgia Power, such as monthly netting.
  • A lease or PPA beats buying. Third-party owners can still use the commercial tax credit. If a provider offers a low, fixed rate with no steep escalator, the math can beat a cash purchase. Compare with our lease vs. buy guide.

Georgia Solar Incentives in 2026

Georgia has no state solar tax credit, no statewide rebate, and no SREC market. The federal homeowner credit ended after 2025. Some local governments and EMCs run small programs, so search DSIRE by ZIP code. If you live in an HOA community, read our HOA guide before you plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Georgia have net metering?

Not statewide. Georgia Power uses instantaneous netting, which credits exports at a low avoided-cost rate. Some EMCs and municipal utilities offer their own net metering or monthly netting.

How much does Georgia Power pay for solar?

Its 2026 solar avoided-cost rate is about 3.2¢ per kWh, with some analyses adding a rate-case adder that brings the effective credit near 7¢. Either is far below the retail price.

How long does solar take to pay off in Georgia?

Roughly 15 to 18 years for a typical purchased system on Georgia Power, less for homes with high daytime use or better utility terms.

Is a solar lease a good idea in Georgia?

It can be, because the leasing company can still claim a federal tax credit that homeowners cannot. Check the monthly price, the yearly escalator, and what happens if you sell the house.
