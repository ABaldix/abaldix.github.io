Is solar worth it in Michigan? It can be, mostly because electricity is expensive. Michigan is not a sunny state, and it replaced net metering with an "inflow/outflow" system that pays less for power you export. But at more than 20 cents per kWh, each kWh your panels replace is worth a lot, and a well-designed system can pay for itself over its lifetime.

How Inflow/Outflow Billing Works

Michigan's regulators replaced net metering with the Distributed Generation (DG) program, and a 2023 clean energy law expanded how much solar the program can accept. Your meter tracks two flows:

  • Inflow: electricity you draw from the grid, billed at your normal retail rate.
  • Outflow: excess solar you send to the grid, credited at a lower outflow rate.

The outflow credit is based mainly on the power supply part of the rate, so it is below retail. It differs by utility and rate plan: DTE's flat DG rate and Consumers Energy's time-of-use DG rate produce different credits. As a rule of thumb, exported solar is worth roughly half of what solar you use yourself is worth. Ask your installer to model your exact tariff.

Making Solar Work in Michigan

  • Raise self-consumption. Electric heat pumps, EV charging, and daytime appliance use all increase the share of solar worth full retail.
  • Consider a battery. Storing midday solar for evening use avoids low outflow credits and covers outages from summer storms and ice. See solar with or without a battery.
  • Expect seasonal swings. Michigan systems produce far more in summer than winter. Snow slides off tilted panels within a few sunny days, and cold improves efficiency; read how panels handle snow.
  • Get several quotes. Prices vary a lot between installers. Use our quote checklist.

Michigan Solar Incentives in 2026

Michigan exempts the added value of residential solar from property tax under a 2023 law. There is no state income tax credit, and the federal homeowner credit ended after 2025. Some utilities and cities run green power or efficiency programs, and leases or PPAs can still pass through the commercial federal credit. Search DSIRE by ZIP code for local programs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Michigan have net metering?

Not classic net metering for new customers. Michigan uses inflow/outflow billing, where exported solar earns an outflow credit below the retail rate.

How much do DTE and Consumers pay for exported solar?

Outflow credits depend on your utility and rate plan and are generally somewhere around 8 to 14 cents per kWh, below the retail price.

Do solar panels work in Michigan winters?

Yes, though output is much lower in winter. Panels shed snow quickly and work efficiently in the cold; most of the year's savings come from late spring to early fall.

How long does solar take to pay off in Michigan?

About 13 to 15 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, faster with high self-consumption or lower installed prices.
