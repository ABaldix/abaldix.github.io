If you're thinking about adding a battery now or in the future, the type of inverter you choose matters. A standard grid-tie inverter does one job; a hybrid inverter does two. Here's the difference and how to choose.

Grid-Tie Inverters

A grid-tie inverter converts DC electricity from your panels into AC power that matches the grid. It powers your home first and sends any surplus to the grid for net metering credit. It has no battery management functions and, by design, shuts down when the grid goes down. String inverters and microinverters in solar-only systems are grid-tie inverters.

Hybrid Inverters

A hybrid inverter (also called a multi-mode inverter) combines a solar inverter and a battery inverter-charger in one unit. It can:

  • Convert solar DC to AC for your home.
  • Charge a battery directly from the panels, without converting to AC and back.
  • Discharge the battery to power your home in the evening or during peak rates.
  • Island your home during outages (with a transfer switch or gateway) to provide backup power.

Some batteries now include a hybrid inverter inside, as the Tesla Powerwall 3 does. See our Powerwall 3 review.

AC Coupling vs. DC Coupling

DC-coupled (hybrid inverter)AC-coupled (separate battery inverter)
How it worksPanels and battery share one inverter on the DC sideBattery has its own inverter, connected on the AC side
EfficiencyHigher; fewer conversionsSlightly lower; solar is converted to AC and back to DC
Best forNew solar + storage systemsAdding a battery to an existing system
Equipment costOften lower for new installsHigher; two inverters
FlexibilityBattery must be compatible with the inverterWorks with almost any existing solar system

Costs

A hybrid inverter typically costs more than a basic string inverter of the same size, often by a few hundred to a couple of thousand dollars installed. If you never add a battery, that premium buys little. If you do, it usually costs less than adding a separate battery inverter later.

How to Choose

  • Solar only, no battery plans: a standard string inverter, optimizers, or microinverters. See the comparison.
  • Solar + battery now: a hybrid inverter or a battery with a built-in inverter (DC-coupled).
  • Battery in the next few years: a hybrid inverter or a confirmed battery-ready design.
  • Existing system, adding a battery: usually an AC-coupled battery.

Questions to Ask Your Installer

  1. Which batteries is this inverter compatible with, and are they available locally?
  2. What is the inverter's continuous and surge power in backup mode?
  3. Will it back up the whole home or only selected circuits?
  4. Does it meet rapid shutdown requirements on its own?
  5. What's the warranty, and can it be extended?

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a hybrid inverter work without a battery?

Yes. It works as a normal grid-tie inverter until you add storage. Some models offer limited daytime backup without a battery.

Do microinverters work with batteries?

Yes, through AC-coupled batteries designed for them, such as the Enphase IQ Battery.
