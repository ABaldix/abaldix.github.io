Is solar worth it in Utah? It depends heavily on your utility and how you design the system. Utah has excellent sunshine, but electricity is cheap and Rocky Mountain Power, which serves most homes, credits exported solar at only a few cents per kWh. The state's residential solar tax credit has also expired. Solar can still make sense, but it is a long-term investment here, not a quick payback.

How Rocky Mountain Power Net Billing Works

New Rocky Mountain Power solar customers are on Schedule 137, Net Billing Service. Solar first powers your home, offsetting your full retail price. Anything you export earns an export credit rate that is set annually on March 1, with a higher rate in the summer billing months (June to September) than the rest of the year. Recent rates have been roughly 4 to 6 cents per kWh. Unused credits do not carry forward indefinitely, so oversizing does not pay.

That means every kWh you use yourself is worth two to three times more than one you export. Check the current Schedule 137 rates on Rocky Mountain Power's tariff page or Utah Clean Energy's guide before you size a system.

How to Make Solar Pay in Utah

  • Size for self-consumption. Match the system to your daytime use instead of your annual total. Fewer exports means more value per panel.
  • Shift loads into the day. Run laundry, dishwashers, and EV charging while the sun is up.
  • Consider a battery program. Rocky Mountain Power's Wattsmart battery program has paid upfront incentives for batteries that let the utility draw on them during peak demand, which can make storage worthwhile. Compare options in our battery cost guide.
  • Check your utility. If you live in a city with its own utility, such as St. George or Murray, net metering may be available and the math improves a lot.
  • Look at a lease or PPA. Third-party owners can still claim the commercial federal credit, so a fair lease may beat a cash purchase. See lease vs. buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Utah have net metering?

Not for new Rocky Mountain Power customers, who are on net billing with a low export credit rate. Some municipal utilities still offer net metering.

How much does Rocky Mountain Power pay for solar?

Roughly 4 to 6 cents per kWh for exported power, higher in summer billing months. Rates are reset every March 1.

Is there still a Utah solar tax credit?

No. Utah's residential renewable energy tax credit has expired, and the federal homeowner credit ended after 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Utah?

About 15 to 17 years for a typical purchased system on Rocky Mountain Power in 2026, less with high daytime use, a battery program, or municipal net metering.
