Is solar worth it in Indiana? For most homes it is a long-term, modest investment rather than a quick win. Indiana's investor-owned utilities no longer offer net metering to new solar customers, and the federal tax credit ended for 2026 installs. Solar can still make sense, but only if you size the system around the power you use during the day.

How EDG Works in Indiana

A 2017 state law (Senate Enrolled Act 309) phased out retail net metering for new customers of Duke Energy Indiana, AES Indiana, NIPSCO, Indiana Michigan Power (I&M), and CenterPoint. They now use Excess Distributed Generation, or EDG. Two things make it weaker than net metering:

  • Instantaneous netting. Exports are measured moment by moment, not netted over the month. Any solar power your home is not using at that instant counts as an export.
  • Low export rate. Each utility sets its EDG rate at 1.25 times its average wholesale energy price and updates it every year. Recent rates have been roughly 4–5.5¢/kWh, against retail prices of 13–20¢.

Solar power you use directly still saves the full retail rate. That is why system size matters so much in Indiana. Rural electric co-ops (REMCs) and municipal utilities set their own rules, so check with yours. Read how net metering and net billing differ for the basics.

Indiana Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Sales tax exemption. Solar equipment is exempt from Indiana's 7% sales tax; your installer applies it at purchase.
  • Property tax exemption. The value solar adds to your home is exempt from property tax, but you must file the deduction with your county auditor.
  • Local programs. Some cities, including Bloomington, have offered home energy rebates; check your city and utility.
  • Federal credit. The 30% residential credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025.

Who Should Go Solar in Indiana

Solar works best for homeowners who use a lot of power during the day, such as people who work from home, run pool pumps, or charge an electric vehicle at midday. A battery can store surplus solar instead of exporting it for about 5¢, but at $10,000 or more it rarely pays for itself on savings alone; see solar with or without a battery. Compare with neighboring Ohio, which kept net metering.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Indiana still have net metering?

Not for new customers of the five investor-owned utilities. They use Excess Distributed Generation (EDG), which credits exports at roughly 4–5.5¢/kWh. Some co-ops and municipal utilities have different rules.

What is the EDG rate in Indiana?

Each utility sets it at 1.25 times its average wholesale energy price and updates it yearly. Recent rates have been about 4–5.5¢/kWh.

Is solar exempt from taxes in Indiana?

Yes. Solar equipment is exempt from sales tax, and the added home value is exempt from property tax once you file the deduction.

How long does solar take to pay off in Indiana?

About 19 years for a full-size system in 2026, or roughly 14–15 years for a smaller system sized to your daytime use.
