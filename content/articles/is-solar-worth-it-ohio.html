Is solar worth it in Ohio? For many homeowners it is, though it is a medium-term investment rather than a quick win. Ohio has moderate sunshine, but electricity prices have risen sharply, especially in FirstEnergy territory, and the state still requires net metering. With the federal credit gone, how you size the system matters more than ever.

How Net Metering Works in Ohio

Ohio rules require investor-owned utilities, including AEP Ohio, the FirstEnergy companies (Ohio Edison, The Illuminating Company, Toledo Edison), Duke Energy Ohio, and AES Ohio, to offer net metering for home solar systems. Within each billing period, the solar you export offsets the power you import. If you export more than you use, the surplus is credited only at the generation (energy supply) portion of the rate, not the full retail price, and carries forward.

In practice, a system sized to your usage keeps most of its value, while an oversized system earns little for the extra power. AEP Ohio asked regulators to reduce solar credits, and the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio rejected that request in early 2026, keeping the current framework through its next review. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Shopping for electricity? If you buy power from a competitive supplier instead of your utility's standard offer, net metering terms can differ. Check with your supplier before installing.

Ohio Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Property tax. Ohio law provides property tax relief for qualifying solar energy systems. Confirm how it applies with your county auditor.
  • SRECs. Ohio solar renewable energy certificates exist but trade at low prices, often only a few dollars to low teens per MWh, so treat them as a small bonus.
  • Financing. State-supported low-interest loan programs and local energy loans can reduce borrowing costs compared with dealer financing.
  • No state income tax credit and no federal credit for purchases after 2025.

Winter, Snow, and Roofs

Ohio systems produce much less in December than in June, and snow can cover panels for a few days after storms. Annual totals still work out, because summer surpluses offset winter shortfalls within the billing rules. Older roofs are common, so replace an aging roof before installing. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Ohio have net metering?

Yes. Investor-owned utilities must offer net metering for home solar. Surplus generation beyond what you use is credited at the generation rate rather than full retail.

Did AEP Ohio cut net metering?

AEP Ohio asked to reduce solar credits, but the Public Utilities Commission of Ohio rejected the request in early 2026, keeping the existing framework in place.

Is there an Ohio solar tax credit?

No. Ohio has no state income tax credit for residential solar, and the federal homeowner credit ended after 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Ohio?

About 11 to 13 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility, usage, and how well the system is sized.
