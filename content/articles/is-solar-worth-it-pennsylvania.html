Is solar worth it in Pennsylvania? For most homeowners with a decent roof, yes. Pennsylvania is not a sunny state, but it has two things that matter more: electricity prices that are now well above the national average, and a state law that requires full retail net metering. Together they give Pennsylvania a solid, if unspectacular, solar payback.

How Net Metering Works in Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania's Alternative Energy Portfolio Standards Act requires investor-owned utilities, including PECO, PPL, Duquesne Light, and the FirstEnergy companies (Met-Ed, Penelec, Penn Power, West Penn Power), to offer net metering. Exported solar offsets power you buy later at the full retail rate, and credits roll forward month to month. At the end of the year, any surplus is paid at the lower "price to compare," so size your system to your usage.

PPL has proposed moving new solar customers to hourly, wholesale-based export credits. Check the current status with your installer if you are in PPL territory; existing customers are typically grandfathered. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Pennsylvania SRECs

Every 1,000 kWh your system produces creates one solar renewable energy certificate (SREC), which you can sell through a broker to electricity suppliers that need them for compliance. Pennsylvania's solar requirement is small, so prices are modest and move with the market: roughly $20 to $50 per SREC in 2026. For a system producing 10,800 kWh a year, that is about $200 to $500 a year. SRECs are tracked in PJM-GATS, and your installer or a broker can register your system.

Other Pennsylvania Incentives

  • Philadelphia Solar Rebate. The city has offered a per-watt rebate for residential systems through the Philadelphia Energy Authority. Funding is limited and often waitlisted.
  • Low-interest financing. State and local energy loan programs can lower the cost of borrowing compared with dealer-financed loans.
  • No state credit, sales tax exemption, or statewide property tax exemption. Ask your installer whether sales tax is included in the price.

Clouds, Snow, and Roofs

Western Pennsylvania is cloudier than the Philadelphia area, so expect lower output per panel around Pittsburgh and Erie. Snow slides off tilted panels within days, and cold weather improves efficiency. As in most older housing markets, the bigger issue is roof age: replace an aging roof before you install. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Pennsylvania have net metering?

Yes. Investor-owned utilities must offer 1:1 retail net metering for residential systems, with annual surpluses paid at the price to compare. PPL has proposed changes for new customers, so check the current status.

How much are SRECs worth in Pennsylvania?

They trade on the market, roughly $20 to $50 each in 2026. One SREC equals 1,000 kWh of solar production.

Is there a Pennsylvania solar tax credit?

No. Pennsylvania has no state solar tax credit, and the federal homeowner credit ended after 2025.

How long does solar take to pay off in Pennsylvania?

About 9 to 12 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility rate, installed price, and SREC sales.
