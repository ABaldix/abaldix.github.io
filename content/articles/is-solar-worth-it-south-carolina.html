Is solar worth it in South Carolina? For homeowners who pay state income tax, it often is, thanks to one of the most generous state solar tax credits left in the country. South Carolina has good sunshine and moderate electricity prices, and since the federal homeowner credit ended, the 25% state credit has become the single biggest factor in the math.

The South Carolina Solar Tax Credit

South Carolina's credit, claimed on Schedule TC-38, equals 25% of the cost of a solar system on your home. Each year you can use up to $3,500 or 50% of your state income tax liability, whichever is less, and carry the rest forward for up to 10 years. On a $21,500 system, the credit is worth about $5,400, typically used over two years if your state tax bill is large enough. Confirm your situation with a tax professional, especially if you are retired or your state tax is small.

How Solar Choice Rates Work

South Carolina's 2019 Energy Freedom Act led Duke Energy and Dominion Energy South Carolina to adopt "Solar Choice" tariffs for new solar customers. They combine time-of-use pricing with netting rules, so solar is most valuable when it offsets power you use in the same time period. Excess generation is credited at a lower rate. The details differ by utility and change through regulatory proceedings, so ask your installer to model your exact tariff. Santee Cooper and electric co-ops set their own rules. Other South Carolina Incentives

  • Property tax exemption. Residential solar systems are exempt from property tax, so the added value does not raise your bill.
  • Utility rebates. Santee Cooper has offered per-watt rebates for its customers, subject to funding. Duke and Dominion do not currently offer solar rebates in South Carolina.
  • Batteries. Because Solar Choice rewards self-consumption, a battery can raise the value of your solar and keep the lights on during hurricanes. See solar with or without a battery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does South Carolina still have a solar tax credit?

Yes. The state credit equals 25% of the cost, usable up to $3,500 a year or half your state tax liability, with a 10-year carryforward. It is separate from the federal homeowner credit, which ended after 2025.

Does South Carolina have net metering?

Duke Energy and Dominion Energy South Carolina use Solar Choice tariffs, which credit solar within time-of-use periods and pay a lower rate for excess. It is not classic 1:1 net metering, but it still values self-consumed solar at retail.

How long does solar take to pay off in South Carolina?

About 10 years with the state tax credit for a typical purchased system in 2026, and about 13 years without it.

Can I use the state credit if I lease?

Generally the credit goes to the system owner, so it is designed for purchased systems. Ask your tax professional and the leasing company how it is handled.
