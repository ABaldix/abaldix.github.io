Is solar worth it in Minnesota? For most homeowners with a sunny roof, yes, but it is a slow, steady investment rather than a quick win. Minnesota does not have desert sun, yet its net metering law is unusually strong: every utility must credit your surplus at the retail rate. With the 30% federal credit gone for 2026 installs, that law and the state's tax exemptions carry most of the math.

How Net Metering Works in Minnesota

Minnesota's net metering statute covers investor-owned utilities like Xcel Energy, Minnesota Power, and Otter Tail Power, and also electric co-ops and municipal utilities. For systems under 40 kW, which includes nearly every home, the energy you send to the grid is credited at the utility's average retail rate. That is far better than the low "avoided cost" rates used in many neighboring states. Co-ops and municipal utilities can add a monthly fee for solar customers, so ask for your utility's distributed generation tariff before you sign. Lawmakers debated cutting net metering for co-ops in 2025, but the change was left out of the final energy bill. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Minnesota Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Xcel Energy Solar*Rewards. Xcel customers can earn a per-kWh payment on their solar production for 10 years, reported at about 3¢/kWh in recent program years. Income-qualified households can get a large upfront incentive instead, though that track has had waitlists. Your installer applies for you, and capacity is limited each year.
  • Sales tax exemption. Solar panels and related equipment are exempt from Minnesota sales tax.
  • Property tax exemption. The value solar adds to your home is not taxed.
  • Battery rebates. Minnesota has offered residential storage rebates, reported at $175 per kWh (up to $5,000) for Xcel customers and $250 per kWh (up to $7,000) for other utilities' customers, with higher amounts for income-qualified homes. Funds are limited, so confirm availability.
  • Federal credit. The 30% residential credit ended for systems installed after December 31, 2025. Leases and power purchase agreements may still pass along a commercial credit in a lower price.

Does Snow Ruin Solar in Minnesota?

Less than most people expect. Panels lose output when snow covers them, but tilted glass sheds snow quickly on sunny days, and cold temperatures actually make panels more efficient. Minnesota systems produce most of their energy from April through September, and retail-rate net metering lets summer surplus offset winter bills. Do not climb on an icy roof to clear panels; a soft roof rake from the ground is the most you should do.

Who Should Go Solar in Minnesota

Solar is a strong fit if you own your home, plan to stay at least 10 years, and have an unshaded south, east, or west roof. Xcel customers in the Twin Cities get the extra Solar*Rewards payment, while co-op members should check their utility's fees first. A battery adds backup for winter storms and may qualify for a state rebate; see how much a solar battery costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Minnesota have net metering?

Yes. State law requires all utilities, including co-ops and municipal utilities, to offer net metering for systems under 40 kW, with surplus credited at the average retail rate.

How much does Xcel Solar*Rewards pay?

It pays a per-kWh production incentive for 10 years, reported at about 3¢/kWh in recent years, with a separate upfront incentive for income-qualified customers. Rates and capacity change each program year.

Does Minnesota have a solar tax credit?

No state income tax credit, but solar equipment is exempt from sales tax and the added home value is exempt from property tax.

How long does solar take to pay off in Minnesota?

About 10 to 12 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility, roof, and whether you get Solar*Rewards.
