Is solar worth it in Kentucky? It can be, but the payback is long. Kentucky has some of the cheapest electricity in the country, and since the 2019 Net Metering Act each utility sets its own credit for exported solar. Good news came in 2026: regulators kept LG&E and KU export credits at about 7¢ instead of cutting them. Even so, without the federal credit most systems take 15 years or more to pay off.

How Net Metering Works in Kentucky

Kentucky's 2019 Net Metering Act let each utility ask the Public Service Commission (PSC) to set a new export rate for new solar customers. Customers who installed before their utility's new rate took effect keep the old one-to-one credits for 25 years.

  • LG&E and KU (NMS-2). Solar you use at home saves the full retail rate, and exported energy earns a fixed credit, about 7.1¢/kWh at LG&E and 7.5¢/kWh at KU. In its 2025 rate case, KU asked to cut its credit to about 3.9¢. The PSC's February 2026 orders left both credits at their current levels.
  • Duke Energy Kentucky moved new customers to a Net Metering II rider with a lower export rate starting in 2025.
  • Kentucky Power and rural co-ops have their own export rates, so check your utility's current tariff.

Who Should Go Solar in Kentucky

Kentucky solar suits homeowners who plan to stay 20 years or more, want to lock in part of their power cost as rates rise, or value backup power with a battery. Because Kentucky's export credit is fixed in cents rather than tied to retail rates, using more of your solar at home pays off. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Kentucky still have net metering?

Yes, but new customers of most investor-owned utilities get a reduced export credit instead of one-to-one credits. At LG&E and KU it is about 7.1¢ and 7.5¢ per kWh.

Did KU cut its solar credit in 2026?

No. KU proposed cutting it to about 3.9¢/kWh, but the PSC's February 2026 orders kept the LG&E and KU export rates at their current levels.

Are there solar incentives in Kentucky?

There is no state tax credit or rebate for residential solar, and the federal credit ended for 2026 installs.

How long does solar take to pay off in Kentucky?

About 15–18 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility and system size.
