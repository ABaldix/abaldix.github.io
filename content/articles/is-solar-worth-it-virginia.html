Is solar worth it in Virginia? For most homeowners with a sunny roof, yes, and 2026 made the case clearer. Dominion Energy asked regulators to sharply cut the value of new solar customers' credits, and the State Corporation Commission said no. Virginia keeps yearly net metering, has electricity prices near the national average, and offers SREC income on top.

What the 2026 SCC Decision Means

The 2020 Virginia Clean Economy Act required regulators to review net metering for Dominion and Appalachian Power. Dominion proposed a "NEM 2.0" that would have credited exports at a much lower value on a 30-minute basis. In its spring 2026 order, the SCC rejected that approach. Under the NEM 2.0 tariff for new Dominion customers:

  • Solar you export still offsets the kWh you import, counted over a 12-month netting period, which works like classic net metering.
  • Any surplus left at the end of the 12 months is paid at a new rate of about 5.8¢ per kWh, higher than the old avoided-cost payment.
  • There is a new $1 monthly administrative fee.
  • Existing net metering customers keep their current terms.

Appalachian Power's review was decided separately in 2025, and electric cooperatives have their own rules. Read how net metering works for the basics.

Virginia Solar Incentives in 2026

  • SRECs. Virginia's clean energy standard includes a carve-out for small solar, so systems can earn and sell SRECs, recently trading at roughly $22 to $35 each. That is roughly $250 to $400 a year for a typical home system.
  • Property tax exemption. Virginia lets localities exempt solar equipment from property tax, and most do, fully or partially. Check with your city or county.
  • Income-qualified programs. Dominion and state programs offer no-cost or low-cost solar for some income-qualified households.
  • No state tax credit and no federal credit for purchases after 2025. Leases and PPAs can still pass through the commercial credit; see lease vs. buy.

Who Should Go Solar in Virginia

Solar is a good fit for homeowners with a south-, east-, or west-facing roof in good condition, steady year-round electricity use, and plans to stay at least ten years. Frequently Asked Questions

Does Virginia still have net metering in 2026?

Yes. The SCC kept energy-based net metering with a 12-month netting period for new Dominion customers under NEM 2.0, adding a $1 monthly fee and a new 5.8¢ payment for year-end surplus.

Are Virginia SRECs worth it?

They add a modest income stream, roughly $250 to $400 a year for a typical home system at recent prices. Your installer or a broker can register your system.

Do solar panels raise property taxes in Virginia?

Usually not. Most Virginia localities exempt solar equipment fully or partially from property tax, but it is a local option, so confirm with your county or city.

How long does solar take to pay off in Virginia?

About 9.5 to 12 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your rate, installed price, and SREC sales.
