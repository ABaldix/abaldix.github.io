How We Research Our Solar Guides

We do not sell or install solar, so our only job is to give you accurate, checkable information. This page explains where our numbers come from, how we build the payback examples in our guides and calculators, and how we keep them current.

The Sources We Rely On

TopicPrimary sources
Electricity pricesU.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), Electric Power Monthly, average residential price by state
Solar production and sun hoursNational Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) solar resource data and the PVWatts model
Federal tax rulesIRS guidance and the text of federal law
Net metering and export creditsState utility commissions (orders and tariffs) and the utilities' own tariff schedules
State and local incentivesState energy offices, program administrators, and the DSIRE incentives database
Equipment specificationsManufacturer datasheets and warranty documents
Market pricesPublished installed-price surveys and marketplace data, shown as ranges

When an official source is not available, for example for the current market price of SRECs, we cross-check several reputable industry sources and say so in the article. Every guide lists its main sources at the end.

How We Build Payback Examples

To make states comparable, our state guides use the same model as our solar calculator:

  • A home using 900 kWh a month, close to the US average.
  • An installed price of $2.85 per watt before incentives, with no federal homeowner credit (it ended after 2025).
  • The state's average residential price, 0.5% yearly panel degradation, and 3% yearly electricity price increases.
  • About 40% of solar used on site without a battery, with exports valued at the state's typical credit.
  • State incentives only when they are available to a typical homeowner, shown separately when they depend on your tax situation.

These are illustrations, not quotes. Your roof, shading, utility rate plan, and installer pricing can change the result a lot, which is why we always recommend comparing several written proposals.

How We Keep Guides Current

Solar policy changes often. We track utility commission decisions, state legislation, and program funding, and we update affected articles when something changes. Each guide shows the date it was last reviewed. If a program's funding or status is uncertain, we say so and point you to the official page to confirm.

What We Will Not Do

  • Accept payment for coverage, rankings, or reviews.
  • Invent statistics, quotes, or customer testimonials.
  • Present estimates as guaranteed savings.
