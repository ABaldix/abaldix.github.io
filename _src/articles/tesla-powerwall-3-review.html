The Tesla Powerwall is the best-known home battery in the US, and the third generation is a significant redesign. It combines the battery and a solar inverter in one unit, which simplifies installation. In this Tesla Powerwall 3 review, we cover how it performs, what it costs in 2026, and whether it is the right battery for you.

What's New Compared With Powerwall 2

  • Integrated solar inverter: panels can connect directly to the Powerwall 3, so a new solar-plus-storage system may not need a separate inverter. That saves money and wall space.
  • Much more power: up to 11.5 kW continuous, compared with 5 kW on the Powerwall 2. More power means you can run large appliances, such as central AC or a well pump, at the same time.
  • LFP chemistry: lithium iron phosphate is more thermally stable and tolerates more charge cycles than the NMC chemistry used in the Powerwall 2.

How Much Does the Powerwall 3 Cost?

Installed prices typically range from about $13,000 to $16,500 for one unit, including the Gateway and labor. EnergySage reported a typical installed cost of about $13,500 on its marketplace in 2026, below the average for all batteries. Electrical panel upgrades, long wire runs, or multiple units add cost.

The federal 30% credit that covered home batteries ended on December 31, 2025, so a purchased Powerwall installed in 2026 gets no federal credit. State and utility programs can still help: California's SGIP is the best known, and several utilities pay battery owners to share stored energy during peak demand ("virtual power plant" programs). How Long Will a Powerwall 3 Run Your Home?

It depends on what you power. The average US home uses about 30 kWh per day, so one Powerwall won't run everything for a full day without solar recharging. Typical estimates:

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Strong power output for its capacity.
  • Integrated inverter lowers cost for new solar-plus-storage systems.
  • Well-regarded app and wide installer network.
  • Competitive price per kWh.

Cons

  • Fixed 13.5 kWh unit; less granular sizing than modular batteries.
  • Adding it to an existing solar system can be less seamless, depending on your current inverter.
  • Installation and service depend on Tesla-certified installers.

Alternatives to Consider

  • Enphase IQ Battery 5P: modular 5 kWh units; a natural fit with Enphase microinverter systems.
  • FranklinWH aPower 2: about 15 kWh with strong whole-home backup features.
  • SolarEdge Home Battery, LG, and others: often paired with those brands' inverters.

Tesla Powerwall 3 Review Verdict: Who Should Buy It?

It's a strong choice if you're installing new solar and storage together, want whole-home backup that can start large appliances, or live in a net billing state where storing solar for evening use pays off. If you only want backup for a few circuits and have a tight budget, a smaller modular battery, or even a portable power station, may be enough. See portable power stations vs. home batteries.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a Powerwall 3 work without solar?

Yes. It can charge from the grid and provide backup power, though savings are larger when paired with solar.

How many Powerwalls do I need?

One covers essentials for most homes. Two or more make sense for whole-home backup with central AC or electric heat, or for very high usage.
