If you live in hail country, from Texas and Colorado to the Great Plains, it's natural to worry about glass panels on your roof. Modern solar panels are tougher than most people expect, but hail damage to solar panels does happen in severe storms. Here's what hail ratings mean and how to protect yourself.

How Solar Panels Are Tested for Hail

Nearly all panels sold in the US are certified to IEC 61215 (and the related UL 61730 safety standard). The IEC hail test fires 25 mm (about 1-inch) ice balls at 23 meters per second (about 51 mph) at 11 points on the panel. To pass, the panel must show no major visual damage and keep its electrical performance and insulation.

Many manufacturers voluntarily test to larger sizes. Common enhanced ratings include 35 mm, 45 mm, and 55 mm hail, and some panels carry the Swiss HW4 rating, which corresponds to roughly 40 mm hail.

Independent testing by Kiwa PVEL uses 50 mm hail at higher speeds and shows that panel construction (glass thickness, frame design, glass-glass vs. glass-backsheet) makes a real difference in severe storms.

Preventing Hail Damage to Solar Panels

  • Look for enhanced hail ratings (35 mm or higher) on the datasheet.
  • Thicker front glass (3.2 mm is common) generally resists impact better than thinner glass.
  • Check whether the model performed well in Kiwa PVEL's hail testing.
  • Ask the installer about racking strength and tilt; steeper tilts can reduce the impact energy of hail.
  • Durable panels also tend to degrade more slowly; see how long solar panels last.

Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Solar Panels?

In most cases, roof-mounted solar panels that you own are treated as part of your home's structure and are covered under the dwelling coverage of a standard homeowners policy for perils such as hail, wind, fire, and falling trees. Still:

  1. Tell your insurer about the installation and confirm coverage in writing.
  2. Check your dwelling limit; adding $20,000–$35,000 of equipment may justify raising it.
  3. Review your deductible; many hail-prone states use percentage-based wind and hail deductibles.
  4. Ground-mounted systems may fall under "other structures" coverage with lower limits.
  5. Leased systems are usually insured by the leasing company; check your contract.

Manufacturer warranties cover defects, not weather damage. Some installers' workmanship warranties exclude storm damage too.

What to Do After a Hailstorm

  1. Stay off the roof. Damaged panels can be electrically hazardous.
  2. Check your monitoring app for sudden drops in production or offline panels.
  3. Inspect from the ground with binoculars for cracked glass or bent frames, and take photos.
  4. Contact your installer for a professional inspection; cracks in cells aren't always visible. Our maintenance guide covers what to check.
  5. File an insurance claim promptly with photos, production data, and the inspection report.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can panels protect my roof from hail?

The roof area under panels is shielded from direct impact, which can reduce damage in that area, but the rest of the roof is still exposed.

Will solar panels raise my insurance premium?

Sometimes slightly, if you increase your dwelling limit to cover the added value. Shop around if the increase seems large.
