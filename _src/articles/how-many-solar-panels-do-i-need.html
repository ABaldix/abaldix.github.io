How many solar panels do I need? The answer depends on three things: how much electricity you use, how much sun your location gets, and how powerful each panel is. You can estimate it yourself in a few minutes with your utility bills.

Step 1: Find Your Annual Electricity Use

Add up the kWh on your last 12 electricity bills. Many utilities show a 12-month history on the bill or online. According to the EIA, the average US home uses about 10,800 kWh per year (around 900 kWh per month), but your number could be half that or double it.

Planning ahead? Add expected new loads. An electric vehicle typically adds 3,000–4,500 kWh per year (see charging an EV with solar); a heat pump replacing gas heat can add several thousand kWh depending on climate.

Step 2: Find Your Peak Sun Hours

"Peak sun hours" is the average number of hours per day with sunlight equivalent to full intensity (1,000 W/m²). It is not the same as daylight hours, and cloudy climates simply need a few more panels; see solar in clouds and snow.

Step 3: Calculate System Size

Use this formula:

System size (kW) = annual kWh ÷ (peak sun hours × 365 × 0.8)

The 0.8 factor accounts for real-world losses from heat, wiring, inverter conversion, dust, and minor shading. Example: a home using 10,800 kWh per year in an area with 4.5 peak sun hours needs 10,800 ÷ (4.5 × 365 × 0.8) = 8.2 kW.

Step 4: How Many Solar Panels Do I Need?

Divide system size in watts by panel wattage. With 420 W panels: 8,200 ÷ 420 ≈ 20 panels.

Will It Fit on Your Roof?

A typical residential panel is about 18–22 square feet. Twenty panels need roughly 400–450 square feet of usable roof, plus setbacks that fire codes require around edges and ridges. If space is tight, higher-efficiency panels help. See choosing panels.

Should You Cover 100% of Your Usage?

It depends on your utility. With full net metering, sizing to about 90–100% of annual usage usually maximizes savings. Under net billing, such as California's NEM 3.0, exports earn less, so a smaller system plus a battery is often the better value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many panels does a 2,000 sq ft house need?

Usually 17–22 panels, but usage matters more than square footage. See our 2,000 sq ft cost guide.

Does roof direction change how many panels I need?

Yes. East- or west-facing roofs produce roughly 10–20% less than south-facing ones, so you may need a few more panels. See orientation and tilt.
