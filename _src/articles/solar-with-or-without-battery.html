A battery is the most common add-on in solar quotes today, and it can nearly double the price of a project. Whether to go solar with or without a battery depends less on the battery itself than on how your utility credits solar, how you're billed, and how much you care about backup power.

The Quick Decision Guide

Your situationRecommendation
Full retail net metering, few outagesSolar only; choose battery-ready equipment
Net billing with low export credits (e.g., California NEM 3.0)Solar + battery is usually the better investment
Time-of-use rates with expensive evening peaksBattery often adds meaningful savings
Frequent outages, well water, medical devices, home officeBattery for backup value
Tight budgetSolar first; add a battery later

How Solar Without a Battery Works

A solar-only, grid-tied system uses solar power as it's produced and sends the surplus to the grid. Under full retail net metering, the grid effectively stores that surplus for free, so a battery adds little bill savings. This setup has the lowest upfront cost and usually the fastest payback. Its main drawback: it shuts off during outages. See why.

What a Battery Adds

1. Backup power

A battery with backup capability keeps selected circuits, or your whole home, running in a blackout, and solar recharges it during the day.

2. Self-consumption under net billing

When exports earn only a few cents per kWh but evening grid power costs 30–50 cents, storing midday solar and using it after sunset is worth far more than exporting it. That's why most new California systems include batteries.

3. Time-of-use arbitrage

On time-of-use plans, a battery can cover the expensive peak hours (often 4–9 p.m.) with stored solar.

4. Utility program income

Some utilities pay battery owners to discharge during grid emergencies or peak demand.

Popular choices include the Tesla Powerwall 3. Adding the battery at the same time as the panels typically costs less than adding it later, because the installer can share labor, permits, and electrical work, and a single hybrid inverter can serve both. See battery costs.

Planning to Add a Battery Later?

If you go solar-only for now, ask your installer about:

  • A hybrid inverter or battery-compatible system. See hybrid vs. grid-tie inverters.
  • Space near your main panel for a battery and gateway.
  • Whether your electrical panel has capacity for a future battery.
  • Any utility rules about adding storage to an existing interconnection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I go off the grid with solar and a battery?

It's possible but expensive for most homes, because you need enough storage for cloudy stretches and winter. See how many batteries you need to go off-grid.

Does a battery shorten my payback?

Under net metering it usually lengthens payback. Under net billing or time-of-use rates, a well-sized battery can shorten it.
