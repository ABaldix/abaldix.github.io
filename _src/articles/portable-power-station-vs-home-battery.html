If your main goal is keeping the fridge cold and the phones charged during an outage, you may not need a $15,000 home battery. Portable power stations have become far more capable in the last few years. Here's how they compare with installed home batteries, and how to choose.

What's the Difference?

A portable power station is a self-contained lithium battery with a built-in inverter and outlets. You plug appliances into it directly, and you can recharge it from a wall outlet, a car, or portable solar panels. Brands include EcoFlow, Jackery, Bluetti, Anker SOLIX, and others.

A home battery, such as a Tesla Powerwall or Enphase IQ Battery, is permanently installed and wired into your electrical system. During an outage it switches over automatically and powers your home's circuits, and it recharges from your rooftop solar.

Connecting a Portable Unit to Your Home Safely

Some larger power stations can connect to a home through a transfer switch or a dedicated smart panel, powering selected circuits. This must be installed by a licensed electrician.

Never backfeed through a wall outlet. Plugging a power source into a household outlet with a "suicide cord" can energize utility lines and injure line workers, and it creates a fire risk in your home.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose a portable power station if…

  • Outages are occasional and short.
  • You mainly need a fridge, lights, internet, and devices.
  • You rent, or don't have rooftop solar.
  • You also want power for camping, RV trips, or job sites.

Choose a home battery if…

  • You have or are installing rooftop solar.
  • You want automatic, whole-circuit backup, including well pumps or AC.
  • Outages are frequent or long.
  • Your utility's rates make storing solar for evening use valuable.

Many homeowners start with a portable unit for emergencies and add a home battery later. See home battery costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can portable power stations run indoors?

Yes. Unlike gas generators, they produce no exhaust, so they're safe indoors. Gas generators must always run outdoors, away from windows, because of carbon monoxide.

Are portable solar panels worth adding?

For multi-day outages, yes. A 200–400 W foldable panel can add roughly 1–2 kWh on a sunny day.
