One of solar's biggest advantages is how little attention it needs. With no moving parts, a well-installed system can run for years with almost no intervention. "Low maintenance" isn't "no maintenance," though. A few simple habits protect your investment.

Do Solar Panels Need Cleaning?

In most of the US, rain washes panels well enough. Cleaning helps most when:

  • You live in a dry, dusty area (desert Southwest, agricultural regions) with long rainless periods.
  • Panels have heavy pollen, bird droppings, or leaves that rain doesn't remove.
  • Panels are mounted at a low tilt, where dirt collects along the bottom edge.

Soiling losses are usually small, often a few percent, but can be higher in very dusty conditions. Compare production before and after a good rain: if it jumps noticeably, cleaning may be worthwhile.

How to clean safely

  • Stay off the roof. Use a hose or a soft brush on an extension pole from the ground, or hire a professional.
  • Clean in the early morning or evening, when panels are cool. Cold water on hot glass can cause thermal stress.
  • Use plain water or mild soap. No pressure washers, abrasive pads, or harsh chemicals, which can damage coatings and void warranties.
  • Check your panel manufacturer's cleaning guidelines.

Professional cleaning typically costs about $100–$300 per visit, depending on system size and access.

Monitoring: Your Most Important Tool

Most systems include an app that shows daily and lifetime production. Check it monthly and compare it with the same month last year and with your installer's estimate. Warning signs:

  • Production drops to zero (often an inverter fault or tripped breaker).
  • One panel consistently underperforms others (with panel-level monitoring).
  • A gradual decline much faster than the normal ~0.5% per year.

Common Issues and Fixes

  • Inverter faults: the most common cause of downtime. String inverters often need replacing after 10–15 years (about $1,500–$3,000).
  • Critters: squirrels and birds can nest under panels and chew wires. Mesh "critter guards" prevent this.
  • Loose connectors or wiring: best handled by a qualified technician.
  • Storm damage: check after hail or high winds and contact your installer and insurer. See hail and insurance.

Lifetime Maintenance Costs

Over 25 years, a typical owner might spend a few thousand dollars in total, mostly for one string inverter replacement and occasional cleaning or inspection. Microinverter systems usually avoid the mid-life inverter replacement, since microinverters typically carry 25-year warranties.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to remove snow from my panels?

Usually not. Snow tends to slide off tilted panels within a day or two of sun. See solar in snow.

Does leasing include maintenance?

Most leases and PPAs include monitoring and repairs by the system owner. Check the contract.
