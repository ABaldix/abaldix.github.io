The inverter is the brain of a solar system: it turns the panels' DC electricity into the AC power your home uses. Choosing between a single string inverter and microinverters affects your cost, how well the system handles shade, how you monitor it, and how often you'll need replacements.

The Three Main Options

String inverters

Panels are wired together in series ("strings") that feed one central inverter, usually mounted near your electrical panel or in the garage. It's the simplest and cheapest design, with one main component to service.

Microinverters

A small inverter is installed under each panel, converting DC to AC right at the roof. Each panel works independently, so one shaded or dirty panel doesn't drag down the others. Enphase is the leading brand in the US.

Power optimizers

A DC optimizer under each panel tunes that panel's output and sends it to a central string inverter. It's a hybrid approach with panel-level optimization and monitoring. SolarEdge and Tigo are common brands.

Why Shade Matters So Much

In a basic string, panels are connected in series, so the current is limited by the weakest panel. Shade from a chimney or tree on one or two panels can reduce output across the whole string. Microinverters and optimizers manage each panel separately, so losses stay limited to the shaded panels. Modern panels with bypass diodes and half-cut cells reduce the problem, but don't eliminate it. See our shading guide.

Rapid Shutdown Requirements

Since the 2017 edition, the National Electrical Code (Section 690.12) requires rooftop systems to reduce voltage quickly at the panel level when shut down, to protect firefighters. Microinverters and optimizers meet this requirement inherently; string-only systems need additional shutdown devices. Requirements depend on which NEC edition your jurisdiction has adopted.

Lifespan and Replacement Costs

A string inverter is the component most likely to need replacement during a system's life, typically after 10–15 years, at a cost of roughly $1,500–$3,000. Microinverters usually carry 25-year warranties matching the panels, though a failed unit requires roof access to replace. Factor these costs into lifetime comparisons, not just the upfront price.

Which Should You Choose?

  • String inverter: simple, unshaded, single-direction roof; budget is the priority.
  • Optimizers: some shade or multiple roof faces; you want panel-level monitoring at a moderate premium.
  • Microinverters: complex roofs, partial shade, plans to expand later, or a preference for no single point of failure.
  • Planning a battery? Consider a hybrid inverter or a battery with a built-in inverter. See hybrid vs. grid-tie inverters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do microinverters produce more energy?

On shaded or multi-direction roofs, typically yes, by several percent or more. On a simple, unshaded roof, the difference is small.

Can I mix panels of different sizes?

Microinverters make it much easier to mix panel types or orientations, which helps when expanding a system later.
