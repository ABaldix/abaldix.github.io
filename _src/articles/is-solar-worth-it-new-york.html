Is solar worth it in New York? For most homeowners with a decent roof, yes. New York gets less sun than the South or West, but it pays far more for electricity, and its state tax credit is still one of the most generous in the country. That combination gives New York some of the shortest payback periods of any state, even after the federal homeowner credit ended.

The New York State Solar Tax Credit

New York's Solar Energy System Equipment Credit is worth 25% of the cost of a system on your primary residence, up to $5,000. A system costing $20,000 or more earns the full amount. If you do not owe enough state tax to use it in one year, the remainder carries forward for up to five years. It applies to purchased systems and, in many cases, to leases and PPAs as well, so ask your tax preparer how it applies to your contract.

Net Metering and the Customer Benefit Contribution

Residential solar in New York is still credited under net metering, so exported kWh offset kWh you buy later at the retail rate. Systems interconnected since 2022 pay a monthly Customer Benefit Contribution (CBC), a fixed charge per kW of solar that funds public programs and cannot be offset by solar credits. At around $1 to $2 per kW per month, it is about $10 to $20 a month for a typical home. Factor it into any savings estimate.

What about NY-Sun rebates? NYSERDA's standard per-watt rebate for market-rate homes has largely been used up in the Con Edison and upstate regions. Income-qualified households can still receive much larger NY-Sun incentives. Check NYSERDA's dashboard or ask an approved installer for the current status in your area.

Snow, Clouds, and Roof Space

New York's weather is less of a problem than people expect. Panels still produce on cloudy days, snow usually slides off tilted panels within a day or two of sunshine, and cold temperatures actually improve panel efficiency. The bigger constraints are roof space and shading from trees and neighboring buildings, especially in the city. Higher-efficiency panels help on small roofs. Read how panels handle clouds, rain, and snow and how to deal with shade.

Who Should Go Solar in New York

Solar is strongest for homeowners who owe enough state income tax to use the credit within five years, have a south-, east-, or west-facing roof with little shade, and plan to stay at least seven to eight years. Renters and people with unsuitable roofs can often join community solar for a guaranteed bill discount with no installation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does New York still have net metering?

Yes, for residential systems. Exports are credited at retail, but systems interconnected since 2022 pay a fixed monthly Customer Benefit Contribution based on system size.

Can I claim the New York solar credit if I lease?

In many cases, yes, because the credit can apply to amounts paid under a lease or PPA. The rules are specific, so confirm with the Department of Taxation and Finance guidance or a tax professional.

Do solar panels raise property taxes in New York?

Usually not. State law provides a 15-year property tax exemption for the added value, although individual localities can opt out. New York City has its own solar property tax abatement.

How long does solar take to pay off in New York?

About 6 to 9 years for a typical purchased system in 2026, depending on your utility rate, installed price, and how quickly you can use the state credit.
