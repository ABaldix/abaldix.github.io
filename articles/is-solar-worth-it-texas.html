Is Solar Worth It in Texas in 2026? Buyback Plans, Costs, and Payback

Texas has strong sun and low installation prices but no statewide net metering. Whether solar pays depends mostly on the buyback plan or utility you are on. Here is the 2026 math.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Texas has no statewide net metering; in most of the state, a retail electric provider's solar buyback plan sets what your exported power is worth.
  • With a plan that credits exports near retail, a typical system pays back in about 10 years; with a low buyback rate it can take 15 or more.
  • The 100% property tax exemption (Form 50-123) applies statewide; Austin Energy adds a $2,500 rebate for its customers.
  • There is no state income tax credit and no federal credit for purchases after 2025, so a careful quote and plan choice matter.

Is solar worth it in Texas? It can be, but Texas is unlike almost any other big solar state. The sun is excellent and installation prices are among the lowest in the country, yet electricity is relatively cheap and there is no statewide net metering. In most of Texas, the value of your solar depends on the retail plan you choose, which means two neighbors with identical systems can see very different payback periods.

How Solar Buyback Works in Texas

About 85% of Texans live in the competitive ERCOT market, where Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, and TNMP deliver power but you buy it from a retail electric provider (REP). Some REPs offer solar plans that credit exported power. The terms vary widely:

  • Retail-match plans credit exports at or near the price you pay, which behaves like true net metering.
  • Fixed or wholesale buyback plans pay a set amount, often a few cents to around 10¢ per kWh, sometimes with monthly caps.
  • Plans with no buyback give you nothing for exports, which can turn a good system into a poor one.

Read the electricity facts label, check whether credits roll over, and confirm the contract length. A great plan today can be replaced by a worse one at renewal, so treat buyback as a variable, not a guarantee. You can compare offers on the state's Power to Choose site.

Municipal and co-op customers: If you are served by a city utility (Austin Energy, CPS Energy, others) or an electric co-op, you cannot shop for a REP. Your utility's own solar tariff decides the export value.

Texas Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Property tax exemption. Texas exempts the value a solar system adds to your home from property tax. Property taxes in Texas are high, so this is worth hundreds of dollars a year. File Form 50-123 with your county appraisal district.
  • Austin Energy. Customers can receive a $2,500 rebate after completing a short solar education course, plus a Value of Solar credit for production. Austin is usually the best-value place in Texas to go solar.
  • Other local programs. Some utilities and co-ops run small rebates or battery programs. CPS Energy's residential solar rebate funding has been exhausted, so check before you count on it.
  • Leases and PPAs. Third-party owners can still use the commercial credit under current deadlines, which can lower the price. Read our lease vs. buy guide first.

Batteries and Backup Power in Texas

Many Texans now buy batteries for resilience after major grid emergencies and hurricane season outages, not just savings. A battery also lets you use more of your own solar, which helps if your buyback rate is low. Expect roughly $10,000 to $16,500 installed for a single whole-home unit. See our battery cost guide and how solar works during outages.

Who Benefits Most From Solar in Texas

Solar works best in Texas for homeowners with high summer air-conditioning bills, a roof with good south or west exposure, access to a retail-match plan or Austin Energy, and plans to stay at least 10 years. Hail is a real risk in North and Central Texas, so confirm your insurance coverage and panel hail rating before you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Texas have net metering?

Not statewide. In the competitive market, retail providers decide whether and how to credit exports. Some municipal utilities and co-ops offer net metering or their own export rates.

Will solar panels raise my property taxes in Texas?

No, as long as you file for the solar exemption. Texas law exempts the added value of a solar energy system from property tax.

How much do solar panels cost in Texas?

Installed prices in Texas are typically at or below the national average of about $2.85 per watt, so a 7 kW system often costs roughly $17,000 to $21,000 before any rebates.

Is it better to lease or buy solar in Texas?

Buying usually saves the most over 25 years if you can pay cash or get a low-rate loan. A lease or PPA can make sense if the provider passes on its tax credit in a low, fixed rate, but read the escalator and transfer terms carefully.
