Do Solar Panels Increase Home Value? What the Research Says

Studies from Zillow and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory found buyers pay more for homes with owned solar. How much value solar adds, why leased systems are different, and how to document it when you sell.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 3 min read

Key Takeaways

  • A 2019 Zillow analysis found homes with solar sold for about 4.1% more on average than comparable homes without it.
  • Berkeley Lab research found buyers paid a premium of roughly $4 per watt of owned solar capacity, about $15,000 for a typical system at the time.
  • Owned systems add value; leased systems can complicate a sale.
  • Keep production records, warranties, and permits ready to help appraisers and buyers value the system.

Solar panels can lower your bills for decades, but what happens when you sell? Several large studies have found that buyers pay more for homes with solar, though the premium depends on how the system is owned, how old it is, and your local market.

What the Research Shows

Zillow (2019)

Zillow analyzed home sales nationwide and found that homes with solar energy systems sold for about 4.1% more on average than comparable homes without solar. On a median-valued home at the time, that was roughly $9,000. The premium varied a lot by city, with larger premiums in some high-cost markets.

Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (2015)

Berkeley Lab's "Selling Into the Sun" study examined about 22,000 home sales across eight states, including nearly 4,000 homes with solar. It found buyers were consistently willing to pay a premium of roughly $4 per watt of solar capacity, or about $15,000 for an average 3.6 kW system at the time.

Reading the studies carefully: both studies are several years old, system prices have fallen since then, and premiums depend on local electricity prices and buyer awareness. Treat them as evidence that solar typically adds value, not as a guaranteed number for your home.

Why Owned and Leased Systems Are Different

The premiums above apply mainly to owned systems. With a leased system or PPA, the buyer is taking on a contract rather than an asset. That can be fine if the terms are good, but it adds paperwork, may require credit approval, and some buyers or lenders are wary of it. In some cases sellers buy out the lease before closing. See lease vs. buy.

What Affects How Much Value Solar Adds

  • Electricity prices: solar is worth more where power is expensive.
  • System age and remaining warranty: a 3-year-old system with 22 years of warranty left is worth more than a 15-year-old one.
  • Equipment quality and installer reputation.
  • Net metering terms: if legacy terms transfer to the buyer, that adds value.
  • Batteries: backup power is increasingly attractive in outage-prone areas.

Property Taxes

Many states exclude the added value of solar from property tax assessments, so you can gain resale value without a higher tax bill. Check your state's rules on DSIRE or with your county assessor.

How to Get Credit for Solar When You Sell

  1. Gather your contract, permits, final inspection, permission-to-operate letter, and warranties.
  2. Export 12+ months of production data and utility bills showing savings.
  3. Ask your agent to highlight the system in the listing and to give appraisers documentation.
  4. Point the appraiser to the Appraisal Institute's Residential Green and Energy Efficient Addendum, which helps document energy features.
  5. If the system is leased, get the transfer requirements from the leasing company early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will solar make my home harder to sell?

Owned systems rarely do. Leased systems can add steps, which is why understanding the transfer terms before you sign is important.

Do appraisers always count solar?

Not always, especially in markets with few comparable sales. Good documentation helps.
