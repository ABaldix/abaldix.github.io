Microinverters vs. String Inverters: Which Is Best for Your Home?

☀️

By the VivaVoltia Editorial Team

Last reviewed: October 2026

Your solar panels generate Direct Current (DC) electricity, but your home uses Alternating Current (AC). The device responsible for this conversion is the inverter, often called the brain of your solar system. When reviewing quotes, you will typically be offered two choices: Microinverters or a Central String Inverter. Which one should you choose?

How String Inverters Work (The Traditional Way)

A string inverter connects a "string" of solar panels to one central box located on the side of your house. Because they are wired in a series, the performance of the entire string is dictated by the weakest panel.

Pros: They are significantly cheaper and easier to maintain since there is only one central unit to service.

The "Christmas Tree Light" Problem: If one panel on a string inverter is shaded by a tree or chimney, the power output of all the panels on that string drops to match the shaded one.

How Microinverters Work (The Modern Way)

Instead of one large box, a small microinverter is attached to the back of every single solar panel on your roof. The DC-to-AC conversion happens directly at the panel level.

Pros: Since each panel operates independently, shade on one panel does not affect the rest of the array. You also get panel-level monitoring on your smartphone.

The Verdict

If your roof faces multiple directions or experiences shading from trees throughout the day, microinverters (like Enphase) are worth the extra cost. If your roof is completely unshaded and faces directly south, a traditional string inverter is a smart way to save money on your installation.

Not sure what your roof needs?

Estimate your system size and payback, then ask at least three local installers to assess your roof's shading.
