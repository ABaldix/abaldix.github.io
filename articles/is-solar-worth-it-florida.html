Is Solar Worth It in Florida in 2026? Net Metering, Costs, and Payback

Florida keeps full retail net metering at its big utilities and exempts solar from sales and property tax. Lower power prices stretch the payback, though. Here is the 2026 math.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Florida's investor-owned utilities, including FPL, Duke Energy Florida, and TECO, still credit exported solar at the full retail rate.
  • Solar equipment is exempt from the 6% state sales tax, and the added home value is exempt from property tax.
  • At Florida's 15.0¢ average price, a typical purchased system pays back in roughly 10 to 12 years.
  • Hurricane exposure makes roof condition, wind-rated racking, and a backup plan more important than in most states.

Is solar worth it in Florida? For many homeowners, yes. Florida is one of the few large states that still has full retail net metering at its major utilities, it charges no sales tax on solar equipment, and it does not raise your property tax for adding panels. The catch is that Florida electricity is relatively cheap, so savings build more slowly than in high-price states like California or Massachusetts.

How Net Metering Works in Florida

Florida's Public Service Commission requires investor-owned utilities to offer net metering to customer-owned renewable systems. Each kWh you export offsets a kWh you buy later at the same retail price, and extra credits roll forward month to month. At the end of the year, any leftover credit is paid out at a much lower "avoided cost" rate, so it pays to size your system to your usage rather than oversize it.

A 2022 bill that would have phased down net metering was vetoed, and full retail crediting remains in place in 2026. Some municipal utilities and co-ops, such as JEA and Gainesville Regional Utilities, set their own rules and may credit exports at less than retail. Check your utility before you sign.

Florida Solar Incentives in 2026

  • Sales tax exemption. Solar energy equipment is exempt from Florida's 6% state sales tax, which saves roughly $1,000 to $1,500 on a typical system.
  • Property tax exemption. The value solar adds to a home is excluded from its assessed value, so your tax bill does not rise.
  • Net metering. Not a subsidy, but full retail crediting is the single most valuable policy for Florida solar owners.
  • Leases and PPAs. These can still benefit from the commercial credit claimed by the owner. Compare total cost carefully with our lease vs. buy guide.

Hurricanes, Roofs, and Backup Power

Florida's biggest solar risk is weather, not policy. Before you install:

  • Check the roof first. If your shingles are more than 10 to 12 years old, replacing them before solar avoids paying to remove and reinstall panels later.
  • Ask about wind ratings. Racking and attachments should be engineered for your county's design wind speed, and permits in high-velocity zones are stricter.
  • Know what happens in an outage. A standard grid-tied system shuts off when the grid goes down. To keep power during storms you need a battery or a hybrid setup. See whether solar works in a power outage.
  • Review insurance. Make sure your homeowner's policy covers the panels and understand your windstorm deductible. Our solar insurance guide explains what to ask.

Who Should Go Solar in Florida

Solar is a good fit if you own a home with a sound roof, run air conditioning much of the year, and plan to stay at least 10 to 12 years. With full net metering, a battery is optional for savings; buy one if storm resilience matters to you. If you are with a municipal utility that pays less for exports, size the system to your daytime use or reconsider storage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Florida still have net metering in 2026?

Yes. Florida's investor-owned utilities must offer full retail-rate net metering, and the 2022 attempt to phase it down was vetoed. Municipal utilities and co-ops can set different rules.

Do solar panels raise property taxes in Florida?

No. Florida excludes the value added by a residential solar system from property tax assessments.

How long do solar panels take to pay off in Florida?

For a purchased system in 2026, about 10 to 12 years is typical, faster if your electric bill is high or your installed price is below average.

Can solar panels survive a hurricane?

Properly installed panels are rated for high winds and many Florida systems have come through major storms intact. The weak points are usually old roofs and poor attachments, so installation quality matters most.
