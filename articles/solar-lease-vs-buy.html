Solar Lease vs. Buy in 2026: Which Is the Better Deal?

Cash, loan, lease, or PPA? How each option works, what changed in 2026 when the homeowner tax credit ended, and the contract terms that decide whether a lease is a good deal.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Buying with cash usually delivers the highest lifetime savings; loans depend heavily on rate and fees.
  • In 2026, leasing companies can still claim a federal business credit that homeowners can't, which has made leases and PPAs more competitive.
  • Check lease escalators, buyout terms, and transfer rules before signing.
  • Leased systems can complicate selling your home.

When a solar salesperson offers "free solar panels," they usually mean a lease or a power purchase agreement (PPA). For years, buying was almost always the smarter move. In 2026 the math is closer, because only one side of the deal still gets a federal tax credit. Here is how each option works and how to decide.

How Leases and PPAs Work

With a lease, the company installs panels on your roof and you pay a fixed monthly fee, typically for 20–25 years. With a PPA, you pay for the electricity the panels produce at a set price per kWh, usually below your utility's rate. In both cases the company owns and usually maintains the system.

Because the company owns the equipment, it can claim the Section 48E business tax credit (up to 30%), which survived the 2025 tax law. Solar projects generally qualify if construction began by July 4, 2026, or if they are placed in service by December 31, 2027. Some of that value may reach you through a lower rate. Homeowners who buy no longer get a federal credit, which is why leases have become relatively more attractive. See what changed with the tax credit.

The Case for Buying

Whether you pay in cash or with a loan, owning the system means you keep all the savings once it's paid off. Owned systems are also more likely to add value when you sell your home. The trade-off in 2026 is that buyers pay full price unless a state or utility incentive applies, so payback periods are several years longer than in 2025. Read whether solar is worth it.

Watch out for loan dealer fees. Some solar loans advertise very low interest rates but add a "dealer fee" of 15–30% to the system price. Ask for the cash price and the financed price side by side, and compare the loan with a home equity loan or credit union offer.

Lease and PPA Terms to Check Before You Sign

  • Escalator: many contracts raise your payment 1–3% a year. Over 25 years, a 2.9% escalator increases your payment by about 100%. A 0% escalator is easier to compare.
  • Rate vs. your utility: compare the year-one price per kWh with what you pay now, including delivery charges.
  • Production guarantee: does the company compensate you if the system underperforms?
  • Buyout option: can you buy the system later, and at what price?
  • Home sale: what are the requirements for a buyer to take over the contract, and what happens if they won't?
  • End of term: will the company remove the panels, renew, or sell them to you?
  • Roof work: who pays to remove and reinstall panels if you need a new roof?

Which Option Is Right for You?

  • Choose cash if you have the savings, plan to stay 10+ years, and want the highest long-term return.
  • Choose a loan if you want ownership without a big upfront payment and can get a competitive rate without large fees.
  • Consider a lease or PPA if you want lower bills now with little upfront cost, don't owe enough tax to use state credits, or prefer not to handle maintenance, as long as the contract terms are fair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell a house with leased solar panels?

Yes, but the buyer usually has to qualify for and accept the lease, or you may need to buy out the contract. This can slow down a sale, so read the transfer terms carefully.

Is a PPA better than a lease?

Neither is better in general. A PPA ties your payment to production, while a lease is a fixed monthly cost. Compare the effective price per kWh over the full term.
