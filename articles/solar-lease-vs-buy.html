Solar Lease vs. Buy in 2026: Which Is the Better Deal?

☀️

By the VivaVoltia Editorial Team

Last reviewed: October 2026

When a solar salesman knocks on your door offering "Free Solar Panels," they are usually pitching a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) or a Solar Lease. For years, buying was almost always the smarter move. In 2026 the math has changed, because only one side of the deal still gets a federal tax credit.

How Solar Leases & PPAs Work

With a lease, the solar company installs panels on your roof, but they own them. You just buy the power the panels generate at a locked-in rate. Because the company owns the system, it can claim the Section 48E business tax credit (up to 30%), which survived the 2025 tax law. Systems generally qualify if construction began by July 4, 2026, or if they are placed in service by December 31, 2027. Some of that saving may be passed on to you as a lower monthly rate.

The downsides remain: you build no equity, many contracts include annual price escalators of 2-3%, and leases can complicate selling your home, as the new buyer must agree to take over the contract.

The Case for Buying

Whether you pay in cash or take out a solar loan (which also often requires $0 down), owning the system means you keep all the long-term savings, the panels can add value to your home, and once the system is paid off your electricity from the sun costs nothing. The trade-off: the 30% homeowner tax credit (Section 25D) ended on December 31, 2025, so buyers now pay full price unless their state or utility offers a rebate. That makes payback periods several years longer than they were in 2025.

Bottom Line

If you have the cash or access to a low-rate loan and plan to stay in your home for 10+ years, buying usually still wins over the life of the system. If you want low or no upfront cost, a lease or PPA can now be competitive. Read the contract carefully, and compare the escalator, buyout terms, and what happens if you sell the house.
