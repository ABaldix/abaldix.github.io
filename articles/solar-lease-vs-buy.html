Solar Lease vs. Buy: Why You Should Avoid PPAs

👨‍🔧

By Alex Sterling

Senior Solar Energy Consultant • Fact-checked

When a solar salesman knocks on your door offering "Free Solar Panels," they are usually pitching a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) or a Solar Lease. While $0 down sounds appealing, it is rarely the best financial decision for the homeowner.

[AdSense Space - In-Article Native Ad]

The Problem with Solar Leases & PPAs

With a lease, the solar company installs panels on your roof, but they own them. You just buy the power the panels generate at a locked-in rate. Because you don't own the system, the solar company takes the 30% Federal Tax Credit, not you.

Furthermore, leases can complicate selling your home, as the new buyer must agree to take over the lease contract.

Why Buying is Better

Whether you pay in cash or take out a solar loan (which also often requires $0 down), owning the system means you reap all the financial rewards. You get the 30% tax credit, the panels add equity to your home, and once the loan is paid off, your electricity is 100% free.
