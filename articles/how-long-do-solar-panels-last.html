How Long Do Solar Panels Actually Last?

☀️

By the VivaVoltia Editorial Team

Last reviewed: October 2026

One of the main concerns homeowners have before transitioning to renewable energy is longevity. It takes an average of 6 to 8 years for a solar system to pay for itself, so how many years of "free electricity" do you actually get afterward?

The 25-Year Industry Standard

The vast majority of modern, Tier-1 monocrystalline solar panels come with a 25-year performance warranty. However, this does not mean the panels stop working on year 26. In reality, solar panels have no moving parts, making them incredibly durable. Many systems installed in the 1990s are still generating power today.

Understanding Degradation Rates

Solar panels slowly lose their ability to absorb sunlight over time. This is called the degradation rate, and it typically hovers around 0.5% per year. By the end of a 25-year warranty, a high-quality panel will still produce about 85% to 92% of its original electricity output.

Inverter Lifespan: While the panels last 25+ years, standard string inverters usually need replacing after 10 to 15 years. Microinverters, however, often come with matching 25-year warranties.
