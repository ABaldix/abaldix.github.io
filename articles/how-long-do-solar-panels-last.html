How Long Do Solar Panels Last? Lifespan, Degradation, and Warranties

Solar panels typically last 25–30+ years, losing about 0.5% of output per year according to NREL research. What wears out, how warranties work, and when components need replacing.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 3 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Quality panels usually last 25–30 years or more and keep producing after their warranty ends.
  • NREL research puts the median degradation rate around 0.5% per year, so a 20-year-old panel produces about 90% of its original output.
  • String inverters typically last 10–15 years; microinverters often carry 25-year warranties.
  • Panel failures are rare; NREL found a median of about 5 failures per 10,000 panels per year.

Solar panels have no moving parts, which is a big reason they last so long. Most don't suddenly stop working; they slowly produce a little less each year. Here is what the research says about how long solar panels last, what affects it, and which parts of a system will need attention first.

Understanding Degradation

All panels gradually lose output, a process called degradation. A widely cited NREL review of nearly 2,000 measured degradation rates found a median of about 0.5% per year. At that rate, a panel produces about 90% of its original output after 20 years and about 87–88% after 25 years.

Premium panels often degrade more slowly. Some manufacturers warrant 0.25–0.4% per year after the first year. Many panels also lose 1–2% in the first year from light-induced effects before settling into the long-term rate; modern n-type cells are less affected.

What Affects How Long Panels Last

  • Heat: NREL notes degradation can be higher in hot climates and on rooftop systems, which run hotter than ground mounts.
  • Humidity and salt air: moisture can attack seals and connections over time.
  • Manufacturing quality: independent stress tests, such as Kiwa PVEL's reliability scorecard, help identify durable models.
  • Installation quality: poor wiring, loose connectors, or damaged panels during installation cause many long-term problems.
  • Extreme weather: large hail and severe wind can damage panels. See hail and insurance.

How Panel Warranties Work

Panels usually carry two warranties. The product warranty covers manufacturing defects, typically for 25 years on quality panels. The performance warranty guarantees a minimum output over time, for example at least 90% of rated power after 25 years. Separately, your installer should provide a workmanship warranty covering the installation itself.

A warranty is only as good as the company behind it. Favor manufacturers with an established US presence, and keep your paperwork and serial numbers.

What Happens After 25 Years?

Panels don't turn off when the warranty ends. Many systems keep running for decades with gradually lower output, and a system with warranty years left can add resale value. When you eventually replace them, recycling options are growing; the Department of Energy is funding work to make solar recycling cheaper and more widespread.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do solar panels last longer than roofs?

Often, yes. That's why replacing an aging roof before installing solar usually makes sense. Removing and reinstalling panels later can cost several thousand dollars.

How do I know if my panels are degrading too fast?

Compare annual production with previous years and with the installer's estimate, adjusting for weather. A sudden drop usually points to an inverter or wiring issue rather than panel degradation.
