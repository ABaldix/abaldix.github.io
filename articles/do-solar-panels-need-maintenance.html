Do Solar Panels Need Maintenance? A Homeowner's Guide

☀️

By the VivaVoltia Editorial Team

Last reviewed: October 2026

One of the best features of a home solar system is how little attention it requires. Because there are no moving parts, solar panels are remarkably self-sufficient. However, "low maintenance" does not mean "zero maintenance."

Rain is Your Best Friend

For most homeowners in the United States, regular rainfall is enough to wash away dust, pollen, and bird droppings. If you live in a state with regular seasons, you likely won't ever need to climb onto your roof to wash your panels.

When Do You Need to Clean Them?

If you live in an arid climate (like Arizona or Nevada), near an agricultural area with high dust, or in a region prone to wildfires (like California), ash and dirt can accumulate. Heavy soiling can reduce your panel's efficiency by 5% to 10%.

How to clean them: Never use abrasive sponges or harsh chemicals. A simple garden hose from the ground is usually enough. If you must use soap, use a soft squeegee and mild dish soap.

Winter Snow: Do not try to scrape snow off your panels, as you could scratch the anti-reflective glass. Because panels are dark and installed at an angle, snow typically melts and slides off on its own very quickly.

Monitoring Your System

The most important maintenance task happens on your phone. Get in the habit of checking your solar monitoring app (like the Enphase or SolarEdge app) once a month. This will alert you instantly if a specific microinverter drops offline or if your production suddenly dips, allowing you to use your 25-year warranty to get it fixed for free.
