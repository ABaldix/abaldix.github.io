Is Solar Worth It in New Jersey in 2026? SREC-IIs, Costs, and Payback

New Jersey pays solar owners twice: once through bill credits and again through SREC-II certificates for 15 years. Here is how the 2026 numbers work and what to watch for.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Key Takeaways

  • New Jersey's average residential price was 24.6¢/kWh in August 2026, well above the US average.
  • Under the SuSI program, residential systems earn one SREC-II per 1,000 kWh produced for 15 years at a fixed price set by the BPU.
  • Full retail net metering, a sales tax exemption, and a property tax exemption add to the value.
  • A typical purchased system pays back in about 7 years, among the fastest in the country.

Is solar worth it in New Jersey? For most homeowners, it is one of the best solar markets in the country. New Jersey combines high electricity prices with full net metering, tax exemptions, and a production incentive that pays you for every megawatt-hour your panels generate for 15 years. Even without the federal credit, payback periods here are short.

How SREC-IIs Work

New Jersey's Successor Solar Incentive (SuSI) program replaced the old, volatile SREC market in 2021. Residential systems go through its Administratively Determined Incentive (ADI) track. Every 1,000 kWh (1 MWh) your system produces earns one SREC-II, and your utility buys it at a fixed price for 15 years. Your installer normally handles the registration.

The residential price was $85 per SREC-II for much of 2026, and the BPU's 2026 program review lowered it to about $77 for newer registrations. Whatever price applies when your system is registered stays fixed for the full 15 years. For a system producing 10,800 kWh a year, $77 per SREC-II is roughly $830 a year, or about $12,000 over the term.

Who gets the SREC-IIs? If you buy your system, you do. With a lease or PPA, the provider usually keeps them and reflects their value in your rate. Make sure your contract states who owns the certificates.

New Jersey Solar Incentives in 2026

  • SREC-II payments through the SuSI ADI program, as described above.
  • Net metering. Exports are credited at the full retail rate month to month. Any surplus left at your annual true-up is paid at a lower wholesale rate, so avoid oversizing.
  • Sales tax exemption. Solar equipment is exempt from New Jersey's 6.625% sales tax.
  • Property tax exemption. The value solar adds to your home is not added to your property assessment.
  • No state income tax credit and no federal credit for purchases after 2025. See what changed with the federal credit.

What to Watch Out For

  • Aggressive door-to-door sales. New Jersey has a lot of solar marketing. Get at least three written quotes and compare them per watt, using our quote comparison guide.
  • Roof age. Many New Jersey homes have older roofs. Replace an aging roof first, or the removal and reinstall cost later can wipe out years of savings.
  • Financing costs. Loans with high dealer fees can quietly add 20% or more to the price. Ask for the cash price and the loan price side by side.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is an SREC-II worth in New Jersey?

The BPU sets the residential price. It was $85 for much of 2026, with a lower price of about $77 for newer registrations after the 2026 program review. The price you register at is fixed for 15 years.

Does New Jersey have net metering?

Yes. Exported solar is credited at the retail rate, and surplus credits are reconciled once a year at a lower rate.

Do solar panels increase property taxes in New Jersey?

No. New Jersey exempts the added value of a qualifying solar system from property tax assessments.

How long does it take solar to pay off in New Jersey?

About 6 to 8 years for a purchased system in 2026, thanks to high prices and SREC-II income. Expensive financing or a below-average roof can stretch that.
