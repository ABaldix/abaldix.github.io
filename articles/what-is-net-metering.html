What Is Net Metering? How Solar Bill Credits Work in 2026

Net metering credits you for the solar power you send to the grid. How it works, net metering vs. net billing, true-up periods, and how your state's policy changes the value of solar.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 3 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Net metering credits each kWh you export at (or near) the retail rate, using the grid like a free battery.
  • A growing number of states use "net billing," which pays less for exports, so self-use and batteries matter more.
  • Check your utility's export rate, true-up date, and fixed monthly charges before sizing a system.
  • Oversizing a system rarely pays off; exports beyond your annual usage are often credited at low wholesale rates.

Solar panels produce the most power around midday, when many households use relatively little. Net metering is the policy that decides what happens to that extra energy, and it is one of the biggest factors in how much solar saves you. Here is how it works and how the rules differ by state.

How Net Metering Works

When your panels produce more electricity than your home is using, the excess flows to the grid. A bidirectional meter records both the electricity you import and the electricity you export. Under full retail net metering, each exported kWh earns a credit equal to the price you pay for a kWh. At night, you draw power from the grid and use those credits.

In effect, the grid acts like a free battery: you "store" daytime surplus as bill credits and "withdraw" it later. You're billed only for your net usage over the billing period.

Simple example: In July your system produces 1,100 kWh and your home uses 900 kWh. You used 500 kWh directly from the panels, exported 600 kWh, and imported 400 kWh at night. Under full net metering you pay for 400 − 600 = −200 kWh, so you carry 200 kWh of credit into August (minus any fixed charges).

Net Metering vs. Net Billing

Many states and utilities have moved away from full retail credit. The main alternatives:

PolicyCredit for exportsWhat it means for you
Full retail net meteringSame as the retail rateBest value; sizing to 100% of usage works well
Net metering with reduced creditsRetail minus some chargesStill good value; check non-bypassable charges
Net billing / avoided costOften a fraction of retail; may vary by hourSelf-consumption and batteries become much more valuable
No statewide policyUtility-specific, sometimes wholesaleAsk your utility for its buyback rate in writing

California is the best-known example of net billing. Its NEM 3.0 Net Billing Tariff cut typical export credits by roughly 75% compared with the old system, which is why most new California installations now include batteries.

True-Up Periods and Excess Credits

Most net metering programs settle accounts once a year at a true-up. Credits roll over month to month, so summer surplus can offset winter bills. At the true-up, any remaining credit may be paid out at a low wholesale rate, carried forward, or lost, depending on your utility. That is why oversizing a system beyond your annual usage rarely pays off.

Fixed Charges Still Apply

Even with a $0 energy balance, you'll usually pay a monthly customer or connection charge, typically $10–$25. Some utilities add minimum bills or charges specific to solar customers. Include these when you estimate savings.

How to Check Your Utility's Policy

  1. Search your state on DSIRE for its net metering rules.
  2. Find your utility's "distributed generation," "net metering," or "interconnection" page.
  3. Note the export credit rate, the true-up month, any system size cap, and fixed charges.
  4. Ask installers to model savings under your utility's current tariff, not a generic assumption.

Are Policies Grandfathered?

Often, yes. Many states let existing customers keep their original net metering terms for a set period (commonly 10–20 years) after rules change. Ask your utility how long your terms are locked in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a new meter for net metering?

Usually the utility installs or reprograms a bidirectional meter as part of interconnection, often at no extra cost.

Can I get paid cash for the solar power I export?

Most programs give bill credits, not cash. Some pay out remaining credits at the annual true-up, usually at a low wholesale rate.

Does net metering work during a power outage?

No. Grid-tied systems shut down in an outage unless you have a battery with backup capability. See our power outage guide.
