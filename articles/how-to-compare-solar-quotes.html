How to Compare Solar Quotes: A 10-Point Checklist

How to compare solar quotes on price per watt, equipment, production estimates, warranties, and financing, plus the sales red flags to watch for in 2026.

VivaVoltia Editorial Team Updated October 30, 2026 · 3 min read

Key Takeaways

  • Compare quotes on price per watt (total price ÷ system watts), not on the monthly payment.
  • Check that the production estimate is realistic for your location using NREL's free PVWatts tool.
  • Look for a workmanship warranty of at least 10 years and confirm who honors equipment warranties.
  • Be wary of pressure tactics, "free solar" claims, and quotes that still promise a 30% federal tax credit for a 2026 purchase.

Two solar quotes for the same house can differ by thousands of dollars, and they rarely present information the same way. This checklist shows you how to compare solar quotes on equal terms and spot the warning signs of a bad deal.

Before You Request Quotes

Have your last 12 months of electricity usage (kWh) ready, know your roof's age and condition, and decide whether you are interested in a battery. Ask for at least three quotes, ideally including at least one established local installer.

How to Compare Solar Quotes: The 10-Point Checklist

1. Price per watt

Divide the total cash price (before incentives) by the system size in watts. A $22,000 quote for an 8 kW (8,000 W) system is $2.75 per watt. This is the single best way to compare quotes of different sizes. In 2026, $2.60–$3.00 per watt is a typical range for standard equipment.

2. System size vs. your usage

Check how much of your annual usage the system covers. Under full net metering, 90–110% is common. Under net billing, a smaller system plus a battery may be better. See net metering.

3. Production estimate

Each quote should estimate first-year production in kWh. Check it against NREL's free PVWatts calculator using your address, roof direction, and tilt. Estimates far above PVWatts deserve a question.

4. Exact equipment models

The quote should list the panel and inverter brand and model number, not just "Tier 1 panels." Compare efficiency, warranties, and whether a battery is included. See best solar panels and inverter options.

5. Warranties

WarrantyWhat to look for
Panel product25 years or more
Panel performanceAbout 85–90% of rated output after 25 years (some premium panels go to 30–40 years)
Inverter10–12 years for string inverters (extendable); about 25 years for microinverters
Workmanship (installer)10 years minimum; 25 years is excellent
Roof penetrations / leaksExplicit coverage, ideally 10+ years

6. Who does the installation

Ask whether the company uses its own crews or subcontractors, whether its installers hold NABCEP certification, and how long it has been in business. A long warranty is only useful if the company is still around to honor it.

7. Incentives listed correctly

Each incentive should be named, with how and when you receive it. If a quote for a system you will own in 2026 shows a "30% federal tax credit," ask for an explanation in writing; that credit ended on December 31, 2025. See the tax credit guide.

8. Financing terms

For loans, compare the cash price with the financed price to reveal dealer fees, and compare APR and term. For leases and PPAs, check escalators and buyout terms. See lease vs. buy.

9. What's included

Confirm permits, inspection, interconnection, monitoring, critter guards, and any electrical panel upgrade are included. Ask what happens if the roof needs work.

10. Timeline and payment schedule

Avoid paying most of the price before installation. A common structure is a small deposit, a payment at installation, and the balance at permission to operate. See the installation timeline.

Red Flags

  • Pressure to sign the same day, or "today-only" pricing.
  • "Free solar" or "government program" claims for what is actually a lease or loan.
  • Quotes that only show a monthly payment, with no cash price.
  • Savings projections built on very high future electricity price increases (more than about 3–4% a year).
  • No licensed electrician or no clear workmanship warranty.
  • Reluctance to put verbal promises in the contract.
Know your cancellation rights. Under the FTC's Cooling-Off Rule, many contracts signed at your home can be canceled within three business days. Many states add their own protections for solar contracts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose the cheapest quote?

Not automatically. A slightly higher price from an established installer with better warranties and equipment can be the better value over 25 years.

Can I negotiate a solar quote?

Yes. Sharing competing per-watt prices, without revealing the other company's name, is a common and effective way to negotiate.
