How the 30% Federal Solar Tax Credit (ITC) Works: Step-by-Step

👨‍🔧

By Alex Sterling

Senior Solar Energy Consultant • Fact-checked

The single biggest financial incentive for going solar in the United States is the Federal Solar Investment Tax Credit (ITC). As of 2026, this government initiative allows you to deduct 30% of your total solar installation costs from your federal taxes. Here is exactly how it works.

What Does the 30% Cover?

The best part about the ITC is that it covers almost every expense related to getting your home powered by the sun. This includes:

  • The solar panels themselves.
  • Inverters and mounting equipment.
  • Labor costs for site preparation, assembly, and professional installation.
  • Battery storage systems (like the Tesla Powerwall), even if installed later.
Crucial Requirement: To claim the tax credit, you MUST own the system. If you sign a Solar Lease or a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA), the solar company owns the panels and they take the tax credit, not you. Always buy in cash or use a solar loan.

How Do You Claim It?

This is a non-refundable tax credit, meaning it reduces the amount of federal income tax you owe dollar-for-dollar. When it is time to file your taxes for the year your system became operational, you simply file IRS Form 5695 and attach it to your Form 1040.

If your tax credit is larger than the taxes you owe for that year, don't worry—the remaining balance rolls over to the next tax year.
